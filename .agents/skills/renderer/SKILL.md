# Battle renderer (TypeGPU)

The stack is **TypeGPU on raw WebGPU**, pinned. There is no three.js, no TSL and no node material; don't reach for them, and don't propose a stack change. Models and textures are **generated by code** (headless Blender scripts and texture recipes), not hand-authored or downloaded art; the few licensed exceptions are named in [procedural assets](references/procedural-assets.md). Both choices were the user's, and they hold.

Read before changing anything:
- the packages' readmes and the root readme's layout section: who owns what;
- the one frame function, for the phase order (don't restate that order anywhere else);
- the reference for your area:
  - [fog and light](references/fog-and-light.md): sight fog, the mask pass, shadows, sky;
  - [procedural assets](references/procedural-assets.md): the Blender and texture pipeline, bundles, the validator;
  - [performance](references/performance.md): how to measure frame cost, and what keeps a frame cheap.

## Seams you must not cross

- **The renderer draws what it is handed:** frames built from one side's observation. It never imports the simulation, never sees its state, and never draws what the side hasn't learned.
- **Every effect has a published cause.** Nothing is invented from a rule.
- **Fog eyes come from the published tick,** never from interpolated positions. A prop the side hasn't learned never occludes.
- **Picking uses the simulation's shapes and the one live camera,** never the drawn model or a readback of pixels.
- **Poses are per soldier,** from each soldier's own published position, velocity and id. Never a formation slot.
- **Every look number is fixture data** its owner validates, not a constant in a pass.

## One owner per concept

- **Find the owner before adding a second.** Projection, the depth convention, the camera uniform, the environment (light, shade, sun shadow), the fog term, the frame targets, the resource registry, the presentation clock, ground-mark geometry and per-kind presentation tables each have one module. When two passes need one concept, refactor to the shared primitive; don't bolt an adapter beside it. Raw WGSL consumes the shared schemas; it never copies their fields into a second struct.
- **Identity comes from the authority.** A drawn thing is keyed by the immutable id the observation publishes, through every replacement, never by nearest position, list order or a lineage rebuilt in the renderer. Match an effect to its owner before matching it to a point. When the authority moves a thing, invalidate what was built from it.
- **Where a shape ends, and what it is made of, is data.** A shader draws the shapes the simulation exports; it doesn't guess an edge the simulation knows. Membership, distance and the rendered look are three claims: prove each one.
- **Update each resource at its own frequency:** every frame, on view change, on data change (deltas only), or once. Nothing allocates per frame on hot paths.
- **CPU-side math uses the `math` package** (load the [`math`](../math/SKILL.md) skill), with preallocated scratch packed into typed arrays for upload.

## Workflow

1. Read the owner modules and the frame function before adding a pass, buffer or pipeline.
2. Define resources first: schema, usage, update frequency, owner scope and lifetime.
3. Pick the phase by what the thing is: compute for preparation; the depth prepass for opaque geometry; the lit, fogged world pass; translucent effects after the world; the fog mask before post; overlays after post, in display space.
4. Test at the seam first (the feed or the packing), then run the narrowest browser scene, then **open the picture yourself**.
5. For a visual change: diff against the pre-change capture on the real route (a no-op "fix" passes every static check), compare against the target, and get an unprimed critique last.
6. For a change with frame cost, measure it paired ([performance](references/performance.md)).

## Composition

- **Depth is one convention** (reverse-Z, clear, compare and format together). It moves as a whole or not at all. The prepass and the colour pass share one vertex stage, so their depth is bit-identical.
- **Opaque geometry never alpha-blends.** Translucent layers read depth and don't write it.
- **A kind of surface is a range of the mesh, not a branch in the opaque shader.** A mesh's indices are ordered by kind once (opaque, cutout, room, glass), each kind draws through its own pipelines, and the opaque path keeps its stages and its cost.
- **A cutout cuts itself where depth is written, and nowhere else.** Its prepass stage answers the samples it covers (a sample mask: a depth-only pass has no colour target for alpha-to-coverage), and the colour pass shades at depth *equal*. With the prepass's greater-or-equal a hole fills in, because the cut surface is nearer than what shows through it. Round the sample count where the coverage is an edge the pixel resolves and dither it where it is an even average: rounding everywhere bands a far grille, dithering everywhere speckles a near edge. Its caster dithers the same coverage, so what a cascade cannot resolve casts its share of shade.
- **Glass of one material needs no sort.** Two panes blended in the wrong order differ by the product of their opacities times the difference of their colours, which is nothing for panes of one glass. Bound what a pane mirrors (turn its shading normal toward the eye, cap its light), or a grazing view turns it into a pale plate of horizon.
- **A finished picture is shown through the environment's `unlit`:** the scene's exposure and the air, and no light of its own. Shading it through the lit path adds a specular veil brighter than a dim picture.
- **A choice made per instance in a fragment reads a flat varying.** A constant interpolated across a triangle comes back an ulp off, and a hash of it flickers.
- **What belongs in the world is drawn in the world,** lit, fogged and shadowed: structures, ruins, wrecks. **Overlays are composited after post,** unfogged and ungraded, and glow by their own halo, never by bloom.
- **Ground marks are paint on the ground; bodies are never painted; callouts are overlays.** One layer per concept: a second layer for the same thing is a split every check then has to follow.
- **A size on screen follows one rule.** Before retuning a width, trace it through screen-space minimums, every layer and post. Check what survives tone mapping at every zoom: a glow can keep its width and lose its brightness.
- **Animation lives in the shader, off the presentation clock,** not in a rebuild. What follows the pointer gets its own small slot, so a mouse move never re-uploads the rest.
- **An effect that belongs to a model part sits on the drawn part,** not on the simulation's point.
- **A line drawn in pieces joins by construction:** additive pieces overlap and fade over the overlap; blended pieces are cut square. Blended layers of one thing draw in layer order, not creation order.
- **Light an effect throws is one bounded list of lights read by the one shade function,** never a sprite per layer. Cull before you cap. Light adds before fog, so fog still owns what unseen looks like.
- **Whatever casts into view from off screen is culled by its shadow,** not by the view alone. A caster can be coarser than what it casts for.
- **Things placed once and drawn many times share one chunk owner.** A far chunk costs no per-instance work. Bounds cover every pose a thing can reach.
- **Draw count follows the layout, not the camera.** A pool streamed with the view is kept kind by kind, each kind one contiguous range, so a kind is one draw however many chunks feed it. Hide a record where it stands (scale zero) rather than split a range round it, and draw two ranges as one when only a few culled records lie between.
- **A thing of many instances is bucketed and tiered whole,** by where the thing stands, so a change of tier never tears it. Choose the tier by pixels per metre when its detail is the same size on every instance.
- **Expand a whole map only at its coarsest tier,** and read what the art's tiers hold before deciding which of them is static. A change of knowledge rewrites the records it touches in place.
- **A population that grows with the battle is capped in presentation,** never in the simulation.
- **GPU residency is a bounded cache of data owned elsewhere.** Size it by what the view needs, and measure the bytes uploaded each frame, not only the bytes retained.

## Platform traps

- **Destroying the root does not free what it created.** Every buffer and texture goes through the registry, and a check asserts counts and bytes return to baseline after resize, rebuild and reset.
- **Compare resource baselines at the same drawn state.** Await pause acknowledgement,
  advance to a fixed tick and await presentation before counting. Attribute a difference
  to its creation/destruction owner; an empty mesh may legitimately release a late-battle
  buffer. Falsify repeated-reset checks with an actual retained GPU allocation.
  During remount, a missing route probe is a pending state; wait predicates must
  tolerate it until the new view is ready, within the existing timeout.
- **Async results can outlive their owner.** Build into a new scope and swap it in whole; free a build that is overtaken or lands after dispose; keep every public call safe after dispose. A replacement that shares buffers with a bake is one serialized transaction.
- **Stats healthy over a black canvas** is a validation warning, a reassigned WGSL `let`, a reserved word used as a name, an attachment mismatch or a NaN. Treat any console warning as a failed render.
- **Pin only the shared camera bind group;** let the library number the rest. Count bindings before adding one: the default limits are small.
- **The typed pipeline path sets the pipeline and every binding again for each draw.** For hundreds of draws that differ only in their ranges, make the first through it and send the rest to the raw pass, ordered so that buffers change rarely.
- **A pipeline with no fragment stage is the depth-only pass.** Never write fragment depth to fake one.
- **Pad uniform structs to 16 bytes** and test the packer against a byte constant kept beside the schema.
- **Use one module instance of the GPU library per page;** two copies silently lose shader functions.
- **4× MSAA everywhere.** Interpolate at the centroid any varying a later screen-space test depends on.
- **Apple GPUs skip resolving tiles a pass drew nothing into,** so a loaded target can show an old frame there; make the background pass resolve too. Their timestamp queries mean something only as a whole-frame total.
- **Keep matrices float32 on the CPU as well,** so picking and drawing agree exactly.
- **Run headless checks on the real GPU.** A software fallback is fine for pixel logic and fiction for timing.
- **Match the shader style of the file you are in.**

## Determinism and captures

- **One injectable clock.** No wall time and no unseeded randomness in a pass; a held clock gives a still capture.
- **Hardware output isn't bit-stable.** No pixel goldens; a pixel check carries a stated tolerance, and changes only with a written reason.
- **A probe reads the last frame drawn.** After advancing a paused battle, draw a frame before reading stats or instances. A readback produces what it reads in its own submission.
- **Work spread over frames says when it is done.** A capture after a camera cut waits for the layer's pending flag, not for a frame count.
- **Judge a change by a paired frame:** the same tick and camera with only that switch flipped.
- **Stats aren't pixels.** Pair every count with a crop that proves the subject was drawn and framed, and derive the camera from live anchors, not hand-picked coordinates.
- **Judge a detail tier at its boundary:** one pose at the distance the tier changes, drawn at the tier before and the tier after. Force the tier through the layer's own thresholds, never a second switch, so the forced picture is what the game draws.
- **A mask view's "not this" is also the sky and the map's edge.** Keep ground behind whatever a mask check judges, or limit the check to where ground is.
- **Checks derive from contracts** (compositing algebra, the simulation's own vectors, CPU equal to GPU), not from a copied constant.
- **A claim about repeating motion needs whole cycles,** including the longest pause, across several seeds.
- **Hold the inputs still.** Don't rebuild, merge or rewrite built files while a browser check runs.

## Taste the user has set

- **References** (copyrighted, never shipped): Defilade for framing and scars; WARNO for fields, hedgerows, trees, sky and fireballs; Broken Arrow for ground framing, fire and smoke, and how a map reads from above; ARMAPHRACT for sharp sight wedges and hatched contacts.
- **Fog must never read as sun shadow, nor shadow as fog.** Seen pixels are never touched by fog. See [fog and light](references/fog-and-light.md) before touching a colour.
- **Every drawn thing is a workbench appearance** with its simulation footprint. No shader-only scenery.
- **A shown wreck shows its fire and smoke,** fogged or not.

## Failure smells

- **Memory creeping across resets:** an allocation outside the registry, or a late async result not freed.
- **Pale or stale blocks after a camera jump:** a tile the GPU didn't resolve.
- **Flicker in a still capture:** an unowned time source, or depth ties.
- **A cost "regression" with no cause in the diff:** machine load or the wrong GPU. Measure paired before reading code.
- **A private projection or camera struct beside the shared one,** or a pass-local constant for something the fixture owns.
- **Terrain and props draw, no building does, and nothing errors:** the page installed appearances without its map's kits. The catalog load holds no kit; a route that draws buildings takes its appearances from its map's own request (`useMapAppearances`), which fetches the kits its templates place.
- **A visual fix that changes camera, light, geometry and pass order at once:** you won't know which one worked.
