# Battle renderer (TypeGPU)

The stack is **TypeGPU on raw WebGPU**, pinned, with `unplugin-typegpu` in
`web/vite.config.ts`. There is no three.js, no TSL and no node material; don't
reach for them, and don't propose a stack change. Models and textures are
**generated by code** (headless Blender scripts and numpy texture recipes), not
hand-authored or downloaded art; the few licensed exceptions are named once, in
[procedural assets](references/procedural-assets.md). Both choices were the
user's, and they hold.

Read before changing anything:
- `packages/*/README.md` and the root README's layout section: who owns what.
- [`specs/done/battle-look/README.md`](../../../specs/done/battle-look/README.md): its invariants and single owners.
- The reference file for your area:
  - [fog and light](references/fog-and-light.md): sight fog, the mask pass, shadows, sky, and never-reads-as-shadow;
  - [procedural assets](references/procedural-assets.md): the Blender and texture pipeline, bundles, the validator, adding a model or prop;
  - [performance](references/performance.md): the benchmark, paired cost, the GPU lock, and what has cost what.

## Where things live

- `packages/renderer-core` is the device, capabilities, camera and projection, the depth contract and allocation tracking.
- `packages/battle-renderer` is the frame and every pass.
  - `src/frame/battleFrame.ts` `render()` is **the one orchestration**. Read it for the phase order; don't duplicate that order in docs or other code.
  - `src/scene.ts` is the `BattleFrame` interface: the whole surface the lab may call.
- `packages/scene-assets` holds the bundle codec, the validator, the loader, the appearance catalog and `blender/`.
- `apps/battle-lab/src` holds the feeds that turn an observation into the renderer's inputs (`poseFeed`, `effectFeed`, `soundFeed`, fog and ground inputs). It also holds the `/workbench` route.
- `web/scenes/` holds the browser scene checks; `web/scenes/_lab.mjs` has the helpers.
- `fixtures/village.json` `presentation` holds every look number: light, fog styles, models' `lod_px`, effects, pose feel, camera, audio. Each block is validated by its owner.
- `fixtures/biomes/*.json` holds the biome palette.

## The seams you must not cross

- **The renderer reads only what the lab hands it** through `BattleFrame`: frames built from the side's observation. It never imports `web/src/battle/sim`, never sees simulation state, and never draws what the side hasn't learned.
- **Every effect has a published cause.** No effect is invented from a rule.
- **Fog eyes come from the published tick,** never from interpolated positions.
- **A prop the side hasn't learned never occludes.**
- **Picking uses the simulation's boxes,** never the drawn model. `liveCamera()` is the one camera for GPU packing and CPU picking alike.
- **Poses are per soldier:** each soldier's own published position, velocity and id. Never a formation slot.
- **Presentation numbers live in the fixture.** A new knob is a fixture field its owner validates, not a constant in a pass.

## Single owners (one concept, one module)

These owners are in the code; find them before adding a second:
- projection: `camera3d.ts`, with reverse-Z and an infinite far plane;
- the depth contract: `depthContract.ts`, plus `worldDepth.ts` for the access modes;
- the camera uniform;
- the environment: `environmentFrame.ts` gives every material the same light, shade and `sampleSunShadow`, and the effects' cast lights through that `shade`;
- the fog term: `FogTerm`;
- the frame targets: `targets.ts`;
- the resource registry: `registry.ts`;
- the presentation clock: `setClock`.

When a new need shows two passes owning one concept, refactor to the shared primitive. Don't bolt an adapter beside the old owner. The camera struct is mirrored by hand in `effectPass.ts`'s raw WGSL; if you touch the camera layout, update that mirror, or better, retire it.

- **Update each resource at its own frequency:** every frame, on view change, on data change (upload deltas only), or once. Nothing allocates per frame on hot paths.
- **CPU-side math uses the pmndrs `math` package** (load the [`math`](../math/SKILL.md) skill): vectors, matrices, quaternions, frustum and shape culling, noise, seeded randomness.
  - Its functions take the output as their first argument and return it, so preallocated scratch keeps camera packing, cascade fitting and instance packing allocation-free.
  - Pack into preallocated `Float32Array`s for upload.
  - No hand-rolled second vector or matrix library. The reverse-Z projections in `camera3d.ts` stay local only because `math` has forward-Z only.

## Workflow

1. Read the owner modules and the phase order in `battleFrame.ts` before adding a pass, buffer or pipeline.
2. Define resources first: schema, usage, update frequency (every frame, on view change, on delta, once), owner scope and lifetime.
3. Pick the phase by semantics:
   - compute for preparation (poses, grass build, fog maps and tile cull);
   - the depth prepass for opaque geometry;
   - the world colour pass for lit, fogged geometry;
   - effects single-sampled after the world;
   - the fog mask pass before post;
   - the rim and overlays after post, in display space.
4. Test at the seam first (a vitest on the feed or packing), then run the narrowest browser scene, then **open the PNG yourself**.
5. For a visual change:
   - byte-diff against the pre-change capture on the production route (a no-op "fix" passes every static check);
   - run `compare-screenshots` against the target;
   - run an **unprimed `screenshot-critique` last**.
6. For a change with frame cost: run a paired cost run, then the benchmark row ([performance](references/performance.md)).

## TypeGPU (0.12.5) gotchas

- **`root.destroy()` does not free what the root created.** Every allocation goes through `GpuRegistry`:
  - `texture()`, `buffer()`, `own()` and `adopt()` register it;
  - `scope()` makes a size-dependent child scope; `slot()` holds a replaceable resource.
  
  A scene check asserts that buffer and texture counts and bytes return to baseline after resize and rebuild. A new owner that allocates outside the registry leaks.
- **Async results can outlive their owner.** Resize rebuilds the post pipelines asynchronously, so:
  - build the new targets in a child scope and swap them in whole;
  - free a build that's overtaken or lands after dispose;
  - skip drawing while it's pending and request a redraw;
  - keep `render` and `set*` safe after `dispose`.
- **`tgpu.initFromDevice` borrows the device.** Several owners make roots on the one device, and that's fine: a root doesn't own the device.
- **Pin only the camera bind group with `$idx(0)`.** TypeGPU numbers the rest itself.
- **Casts for missing types:**
  - `d.invariant` is emitted but missing from the `vertexFn` types, so cast it in (see `frame/geometry.ts`);
  - `texture.write` ignores a typed array view's offset and length and forwards `source.buffer`, so pass an exact-size copy (see `world/textureUpload.ts`).
- **The `~unstable` command encoder works.** One encoder carries typed passes (`pipeline.with(pass)...`) and raw passes (`root.unwrap(encoder)`) interleaved.
- **A render pipeline with no fragment stage works.** Use it for depth-only and caster passes, so early-Z survives. Never write `frag_depth` to fake one.
- **Three shader styles coexist.** Match the file you're in:
  - TGSL (`tgpu.fn(...)((a) => { "use gpu"; ... })`) for new battle code;
  - WGSL-bodied `tgpu.fn` with `.$uses` for fog and the WGSL ported from `~/dev/game`;
  - raw `createShaderModule` only where WGSL is generated per install (the pose kernel) or not yet migrated (effects).
- **WGSL `let` is immutable.** A reassigned `let` invalidates the pipeline, and route stats stay healthy while the canvas goes black.
- **WGSL reserves words it doesn't use yet** (`cast`, `meta`, `static`, `new`, …). An identifier named after one fails the module at parse time; name a varying for what it holds (`castLit`).
- **Pad uniform structs to 16 B by hand** and group scalars into `vec4f` slots. Keep a byte constant beside each schema, and test the packer against it.
- **Bind limits are tight.** The grass build uses exactly the default 8 storage buffers. Count before adding a binding, and prefer vertex-only storage where the fragment stage already holds the fog groups.
- **Fixed at module load:** the cascade count, bloom's numbers and the grade. Changing them means rebuilding the frame, not setting a uniform.
- **`GPUSupportedLimits` exposes prototype getters,** so `Object.entries` returns nothing. Read the named limits. Passes read `GpuDeviceCaps`; they never re-probe the adapter.

## WebGPU on Apple Metal

- **Reverse-Z everywhere:**
  - `depth32float`, clear 0, compare `greater`;
  - the prepass writes depth; opaque colour uses `"prepassed"` (`greater-equal`, no write);
  - alpha-tested impostor cards write depth in the colour pass;
  - water, grass and overlays only read depth.
  
  The convention moves as a whole or not at all.
- **Fixed 4× MSAA** (the count WebGPU guarantees for every renderable format):
  - colour resolves through `resolveTarget` with `storeOp: "discard"`;
  - depth is 4× and sampleable, and compute reads sample 0 as `texture_depth_multisampled_2d`;
  - world varyings interpolate at the centroid, because at a silhouette the pixel centre can lie off the primitive and probe the wrong surface.
- **Metal skips resolves on tiles a pass drew nothing into.** So the sky resolves into `lit` too, or pure-sky tiles keep an old frame's pixels ("pale blocks" after a camera jump). Any new pass that loads and resolves has the same trap.
- **Timestamps:**
  - empty compute passes write none on Metal, so the timer uses 1×1 cleared render passes;
  - Apple's tile GPU overlaps passes, so only the frame total means anything, and per-pass splits are indicative.
- **Keep float32 matrices,** `math` `Mat4` via `createGpuMat4`. Doubles on the CPU made 32 of 32 frames differ and picking disagree with the GPU. Integer-lattice value noise stays precise kilometres out; `sin` hashes don't.
- **Validation warnings are failed renders.** A bad pipeline leaves the stats and hooks alive over a black canvas. Scenes fail on console errors; keep it that way.
- **Headless Chromium must run on the real GPU** (`--enable-unsafe-webgpu`, full Chromium, not the headless shell). SwiftShader is fine for pixel logic, but its timings are fiction.

## Composition rules that bit us

- **Overlays are never fogged, graded or tone-mapped.** They draw premultiplied over a transparent clear on their own 4× target and composite after post.
- **Overlays glow by their own halo, never by bloom.** The resolved overlay is blurred at half resolution and laid *under* it as a premultiplied layer (`O + (1 − O.a)·halo`, alpha held ≤ 1). An additive glow would clamp over white and break the isolation check's algebra; a premultiplied halo keeps it exact. One screen pass glows every overlay, the x-ray included, without touching a mesh builder.
- **Ground marks are paint on the ground; bodies are never painted; callouts are overlays.** Every mark the fixture's order scheme puts in the `world` layer (under the shipped `yellow-orders` scheme: the selection's circles and arrowheads, soldier markers, cover pips on them, travel chevrons), plus a supply truck's reach, the zone and the border, is drawn each frame into a screen-size paint target (`frame/paintedMarks.ts`), at the ground and against the prepass's ground-only depth. The painted ground layers read it at their own point: the ground takes its colour (mostly emissive, pushed from grey by `paint.saturation`, since AgX pulls a bright hue toward white) as its surface; a blade is lit by it, never painted: the paint under it lights only its bottom few centimetres (`paint.grass_glow`, `grass_falloff_m`), so no coloured fringe rises out of a stroke, and a blade fragment over the stroke as drawn (its own pixel) carries the stroke's glow, so no blade breaks the line. The target holds colour over `PAINT_RANGE` 2, so a mark (the selection, the cover pips) can glow past full value. Ground versus bodies is one property: fog's `FogLayer.painted` (`paintedGround`: terrain, grass, backdrop; `paintedFaces`: the water, and a static prop whose body stops no mover, such as a bridge deck, on its up-facing faces). Every other prop, building, tree, wreck, the fallen and units never read the paint, so no stroke can land on a hull. Rejected: a decal pass in the world that pulled marks toward the eye past the grass, which put strokes on hulls at a low camera; and before that, a no-depth-test marker, which drew over house corners. Paint and overlay marks lie at one height, the ground, and no mark mesh carries a lift. They clear the ground they are draped over by depth alone, pixel unchanged, since a world lift parallaxes a mark off the paint it joins: the paint is pulled toward the eye (it depth-tests only against the ground), an overlay mark's clip depth is biased by a share of its distance (`OVERLAY_DEPTH_BIAS`), a few centimetres at the ground camera. A fixed 0.5 m pull once drew a ring across the shins of the soldier standing on it; the orders tour checks no order ink lands on a body above its ankles. The order marks' colour roles (order, selected, soldier) each draw in a layer the fixture's scheme names: paint, or overlay after tone mapping for a true colour that pops over the tone mapper (it is then not under smoke); both are depth-tested, so bodies hide both. A vehicle's marker is its kind's hull half-length plus `orders.vehicle_marker_margin_m`. Checks read the paint as `paintOnly` (the frame with it less the frame without), at the surface.
- **Animated marks carry their animation in the vertex, not in a rebuild:** `WorldMeshes.paintedMarching`'s normals hold (phase, cycles a second, amplitude) and one fragment reads `cam.time`, the presentation clock. So the marks move every frame with the overlay built once per publication, and a held clock gives a still capture.
- **Marks that follow the pointer get their own small slot, not the overlay:** the range ruler is painted through `BattleFrame.setPointerMarks` (a third mesh slot in the paint pass), rebuilt only when its key moves, so a mouse move never re-concatenates and re-uploads every order, ring and contact. The viewport's `onFrame` hands a lazy pointer ray; casting it costs nothing while no ruler is wanted. A line that meets a unit's circle reads the circle the orders draw (`orderOverlay.ts` `unitCircle`, `circleReach` for its arrowhead), never a radius of its own, so the ruler and the routes stop at the same border. A circle is sized from a footprint the observation carries (a hull's half-length, a squad's area, a garrison's building from `GarrisonState.center`/`half`), never guessed from where soldiers stand, a moving squad's ring aside.
- **A model's only highlight is its x-ray colour** (`ModelInstance.xray`, rgba chosen per unit by presentation; rgb packed as a 24-bit integer, exact in f32, alpha apart). Visible parts are never tinted; the selection is a ground marker.
- **In-world UI strokes are sized on screen by one rule:** every mark builder turns its `_px` widths into metres through `strokeWidth` (`strokeWidth.ts`, `presentation.overlay.stroke`): full width at the default camera and closer, thinning smoothly in log zoom to `thin_scale` as the camera pulls out, never under `min_px` on screen or `min_m` on the ground. Sizes that aren't strokes (a ruler tick's length, its end ring's radius) stay `px × metresPerPx`. Marks rebuild only when the zoom crosses a ×1.25 step (`BattleView`). A 2 px line under 4× MSAA splits its ink between the two rows it straddles in quarter-sample steps, so a pixel check on a thin line sums across it rather than reading its centre pixel.
- **An effect that belongs to a model part sits on the drawn part, not the simulation's point.** The sim's muzzle is a rigid point (a mount's catalog pivot and muzzle; a rifle round starts at the soldier's body), while the model is interpolated, recoils and pitches. So a flash asks `DrawnMuzzles` (via `EffectFrame.build`'s `MuzzleSource`) for the drawn muzzle each frame, after posing, and uses the published point only when nothing is drawn. Measure such bugs by projecting the sim point, the drawn socket and the effect's instance into one frame before guessing.
- **A streak drawn in pieces fades its ends in pixels, never in a share of its length.** A round's tail and trail are laid tick by tick, one quad per flown stretch. Additive pieces overhang their ends by a fraction of their width and fade over the overhang, so the joins sum to one line; blended pieces (a smoke ribbon) are cut square, since two over-composited fades never sum to one. Anything drawn "at the head at the tick's end" needs a tolerance: a paused clock lands a rounding past `age / span = 1`.
- **A round in flight is layered, not one line:** a soft wide glow (`min_px` a few pixels, dimmed rather than thinner) over the whole tail and a narrow hot core over its front. A single HDR-bright thin line tone-maps to a white stick at any hue.
- **Blended layers of one thing draw in layer order, not effect order.** Effects draw in creation order, so a trail's ribbon laid by a later tick covered half of an earlier tick's puffs, cutting each at the join. Every ribbon draws before every puff.
- **Continuous smoke is continuous geometry plus sprites for body.** Sprites alone need spacing under their own width, and their count grows with distance flown; a ribbon costs one quad per tick. Share the motion (rise, spread, wind) through one function, so ribbon and puffs drift together.
- **A population that grows with the battle is capped in presentation, never in the simulation.** The fallen draw at most `presentation.pose.corpses.max`; past it the oldest sinks into the ground (the terrain hides it by depth, so nothing opaque blends) and then leaves the list, so its buffers are bounded by the cap. One never drawn leaves without the sink.
- **Light an effect throws is one light model, read in `shade`, never a sprite per layer.** A glow sprite brightens the air round a flash; it never lights the ground, the grass or the hull beside it. So every effect that lights its surroundings (a shot, a missile's motor, a tracer, a burst, a burning wreck) offers a short-lived point light (`light/castLights.ts`), and the environment's one `shade` adds them to every world material (terrain, grass, scenery, models, water, cards), while the effect pass lights smoke with the same lights. A new lit layer gets the light by calling `shade`, and nothing else. Keep the list bounded: the effects offer candidates, and the world's uniform keeps only those whose reach enters the view, and of those the few the camera sees strongest (each costs every lit fragment a loop step). Window the falloff to zero at the radius with an inverse-square heart (`(1 − x²)² / (1 + k·x²)`); the plain window reads as a spotlight's disc. Wrap the facing, or a pool from a light a metre up dies before its edge on flat ground.
- **A light adds before fog, so fog still owns what unseen looks like.** A cast light brightens the lit colour; the mask pass then gives unseen pixels their look, so a light on unseen ground shows as a dim, tinted glow under the hatch, and never lifts it to seen or touches the mask. It can't reveal anything either: only what the side has learned is drawn, and every light has a published cause.
- **Known world geometry is not overlay.** Structures, ruins and wrecks belong in the HDR world, with fog and shadow.
- **Ground cues use the canonical surface height** (`surfaceZ`), never flat `z = 0`. Draping steps must be finer than the terrain relief, or ribbons speckle under grass and ground.
- **Continuous paths are continuous geometry** (roads are painted from the sim's own segments), not gaps cut around occluders.
- **Opaque geometry never alpha-blends.** Translucent decals and overlays read depth; they don't write it.
- **The prepass and colour pass must compute bit-identical depth.** That's the `@invariant` position and one shared vertex stage. A second single-sample depth or a previous-frame Hi-Z disagrees at edges.
- **Instanced props need a base elevation.** Culling bounds must cover every reachable pose: the tank's bounds grow from 9.5×3.7 m to 12.1×12.1 m with the turret traversed.
- **Casters can be coarser than what they cast for.** Trees cast from one tier coarser, because a cascade texel is coarser than leaf relief.
- **Anything that casts into view from off screen is never culled by the view frustum** (the forest).

## Determinism and captures

- **One time source:** `BattleFrame.setClock`, owned by the route. Holding it freezes wind, effects and poses.
  - Never use `performance.now()`, `Date` or unseeded randomness in a pass.
  - Effects spawn only what would still be alive after a clock jump.
- **Standard capture:**
  - 1920×1080, DPR 1, fixed seed and tick, into gitignored `throwaway/evidence/<fixture-id>/`;
  - verdicts record numbers and critique dispositions, never committed PNGs.
- **Hardware Metal isn't bit-stable across runs** for some surfaces, and grass blade depth ties are the classic case. Judge model sheets "less wrong", with no pixel goldens. Pixel checks use the bounds the scenes already carry: `STRAY_MAX`, rounding, margins.
- **A readback must produce what it reads in its own submission.** Buffers the frame owns are also written between frames: the impostor bake packs its own model into the models layer's palette, and card bakes run in the background after every install. A readback that copies a buffer "as the last frame left it" can read a bake's pose, as a fixed, repeated wrong value (the workbench palette check, 2026-09-27). So `readPalette` repacks and runs the pose kernel before its copy, and returns the bases from that same packing. A scene reading `stats()` after an install must also draw a frame first, since only `prepare` updates them.
- **A probe reads the last frame drawn.** After advancing a paused battle, draw a frame (`__lab.frame()`) before reading `castLights`, `effectInstances` or `stats()`, or you read the previous tick's state: a flash still alive there is already dead in the capture.
- **Judge a light by a paired frame at one tick:** the same tick and camera with the switch on and off (`suppressCastLights`), so the before/after isolates the light and nothing else moved.
- **Debug views** (`setFrameView`, one per `FrameView` in `scene.ts`) isolate a stage; `window.__lab` has `suppress*` switches and probes. Readbacks are lab-only, never inside a frame.
- **Stats aren't pixels.** Pair every instance or draw count with a crop or content probe. Make sure the capture frames its subject: derive the camera from live anchors (`projectToCss`, `surfaceZ`), not hand-picked coordinates.
- **Checks derive from contracts:**
  - overlay isolation by compositing algebra;
  - fog agreement against the Rust oracle vectors;
  - the GPU palette equal to the CPU pose.
  
  A check that hard-codes a renderer constant drifts into false confidence.
- **Retune a pixel check only with a `decisions.md` entry** saying why the old bar was wrong. Never loosen one silently.

## Taste the user has set

- **References:**
  - Defilade for framing and scars (default camera 65 m, 0.85 rad pitch, soldiers about 35–40 px);
  - WARNO for fields, hedgerows, trees, sky and fireballs;
  - Broken Arrow for ground framing and fire and smoke;
  - ARMAPHRACT for sharp sight wedges, an untouched seen area, a dimmed unseen one, and hatched contacts.
  
  References are copyrighted and never ship.
- **Fog must never read as sun shadow, nor shadow as fog.** This is the hardest standing rule; see [fog and light](references/fog-and-light.md). The default preset is `dusk`. Seen pixels are never touched by fog.
- **Models are good enough.** Don't iterate on "toy-like" critiques. Fix outright errors (floating, intersecting, wrong scale, missing parts) in at most one round; a focused model pass comes later. New models match today's quality. The wrecks are fine.
- **Every drawn thing is a workbench appearance** with its sim footprint: trees, grass kinds, props. No shader-only scenery.
- **A shown wreck shows its fire and smoke,** fogged or not.

## Failure smells

- **Stats healthy, canvas black or blank:** a validation warning, a reassigned `let`, an attachment mismatch, a NaN instance field, or bad framing. Read the console, then the PNG.
- **Memory creeping across resets:** an allocation outside the registry, or a late async result not freed.
- **Pale or stale blocks after a camera jump:** a tile Metal didn't resolve.
- **A shadow-like dark region in fog, or a fog-like one in light:** see fog and light before touching a colour.
- **Frame-to-frame flicker in a still capture:** an unowned time source, or depth ties (grass).
- **A perf "regression" with no plausible cause in the diff:** machine load or the wrong GPU. Rerun paired under the lock before reading the code.
- **A private projection or camera struct beside the shared one,** or a pass-local constant for something the fixture owns.
- **A visual fix that changes camera, light, geometry and pass order at once:** you won't know which one worked.
