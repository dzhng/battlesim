## Goal

Every soldier in the game wears a body generated by an image-to-3D model
instead of today's procedural cloth shells, and the procedural clothing code is
deleted. The user said: "let's do the entire thing".

## What already exists (a proven pilot, uncommitted in this checkout)

A pilot converted ONE look end to end: US Army OCP, look `a`, kind `rifle`, on
three kits (`rifle_squad`, `machine_gunner`, the US marksman squad's M110), plus
the Humvee's turret gunner. Read these files:

- `packages/scene-assets/blender/generated_body.py`: wears a pinned generated
  mesh on the 65-bone Quaternius UBC rig. It turns the mesh to face -Y (the
  pin's `front`), decimates it to `BODY_TRIS` (22000), and fits its height
  (helmet on) to `HEIGHT_M` (1.72), its fingertip span to the rig body's and
  its torso to the body's centre. It seats each arm and leg on its bones
  (`_align_arms`, `_align_legs`: slice-wise moves, shape unchanged, faded at the
  joints). It takes weights from the procedural slimmed UBC body by
  nearest-face Data Transfer, with an arm vertex weighted only from the arm.
  It cuts the generator's fused hands at the wrist and replaces them with the
  procedural glove shell. It splits skin faces (head bones plus skin-coloured
  texels) into an untinted `generated_skin` material and the rest into
  `generated` (side tint 1.0). It regrades the generator's atlas so the cloth
  mean matches the army's uniform-print recipe mean, and embeds it at
  `ATLAS_PX` 512 through `textures.ATLASES`.
- `packages/scene-assets/blender/skins/infantry.json`: the pin (army, look,
  kind, front, glb path, sha256, generator, request_id, terms, inputs with
  hashes, note). The GLB and its input image are in `assets/source/skins/infantry/`.
- `packages/scene-assets/blender/roster/infantry_equipment.py`: `KITS`
  (24 kits, each naming its weapon, scout flag, armies), `ARMIES` (4 armies:
  us_army_ocp, usmc_marpat, german_flecktarn, eastern_emr; each with a faction,
  a print, kit colours, a helmet, a rifle, an LMG and per-look a/b/c
  variants), and `build()`, which now uses `generated_body.pin_of(army, look,
  kind)` and otherwise runs the procedural `Kit` shells. `infantry_kit.py`
  holds `Kit` (procedural shells, pouches, helmet, pack, gloves, paint, join,
  AO) and the 12 test soldiers' build (`assets/source/test/{rifle,recon,at,at_carried}*.glb`).
- `packages/scene-assets/blender/textures.py` (`ATLASES`, `box_uv` keeps atlas
  UVs, `attach` embeds atlases) and `textures_uniforms.py` (the uniform-print
  recipes and `UNIFORMS`, just split out of textures.py).
- `packages/scene-assets/blender/vehicle_crew.py`: crews are cut from the
  faction's `rifle_squad_*_active_a` (`CREW_SOLDIER` keyed by faction us /
  europe / eastern), tiers 1-3, pieces weighted wholly to hand_r cut.
  Crewed families: roster/humvee.py, lav.py, stryker.py, tigr.py, vbci.py, vbl.py.
- `specs/generated-soldiers/README.md`: the pilot's spec, results and cost.

Pilot facts, measured:
- Generator: Tripo H3.1 image-to-3D through Bento's fal proxy
  (`tripo3d/h3.1/image-to-3d`, detailed texture and geometry, face limit
  40000, orientation align_image), $0.60 per call. The input is one studio
  T-pose front image from GPT Image 2.6 Flare (`bento local model chat --type
  image`), $0.04. Meshy 7.1 (`meshy/v7.1/image-to-3d`, `pose_mode: t-pose`,
  $1.20, front faces -Y) is the fallback. Tripo's multiview mode made people
  two-faced: never use it. Gemini Argon was rejected for soldiers. Calls are
  asynchronous: submit, poll, download (see the `bento` skill's fal reference).
  About 10-20% of generator calls fail transiently.
- All three pilot kits: `asset validate` has no findings, the export repeats
  byte for byte, `asset check` passes, the pinned tests
  (`cargo test -p presentation --test pose_behaviour`, `cargo test -p
  native-client --test models`) pass, all 22 rifle clips are clean in the
  production renderer, and the Humvee gunner works.
- Bugs hit and fixed (lessons): stale Blender vertex-group handles after
  modifier_apply; the arm weight zone catching wide-stanced feet; limbs off
  their bones (drooping arms, knees 2.4 cm ahead of the knee joint), which
  made a corpse's raised knee break the native corpse test (0.6 m), fixed by
  seating limbs; a 1024 atlas plus a 42k-triangle body blowing the load budget.
- Load: the pilot costs +0.7 MiB on the wire (atlas 0.83 MiB; geometry 1.09
  MiB against the old 1.15). "Every unit's art" is 750.4 of a 768 MiB limit

- An unprimed critique scored silhouette 7, surface 6, fittings 6, materials 5

  body a little brighter than the procedural soldiers; the hydration hose
  standing off the vest in the crew's lower tier; no assault pack or whip (the
  rifleman kind's cue), because Tripo dropped the pack from the input image.
- A pre-existing drift: the committed `rifle_squad/active_b.glb` doesn't
  match what HEAD's scripts export.
- Tooling this checkout now has: node deps, the wasm and mechanics builds, all
  catalog sources pulled from LFS, managed Chromium. `node web/asset.mjs`
  validate/sheet/bake/icons/check work. The review sheet (`asset sheet
  <appearance>`) renders contact views, 22 clip strips, the impostor and the
  red/blue side through the production renderer. `/labs/showcase?unit=<kind>&faction=<f>[&yard=1]`
  shows a squad in game. The scratch trial tools
  (`throwaway/soldier-models-trial/compare_render.py`, `fit_and_pose.py`,

## Decisions (user, 2026-11-15)

1. Generator: Tripo H3.1 single image; Meshy 7.1 as the fallback. Keep the
   generator's own texture, regraded to the army's print (not repainted).
2. Looks: the drafter's call. The user said: do what's best, not
   over-engineered, easiest to work with given a generator makes them. The
   lead's lean: each look's input prompt is the army's base description plus
   one short clause naming what sets that look apart (taken from today's look
   table: headgear, eyewear, pack...), plus the kind's cue (the rifleman's
   assault pack and radio whip, the scout's ruck).
3. Delete the procedural clothing and kit builders once every roster look
   wears a generated body. Test soldiers reuse existing generated bodies (they
   are test art, never game content). Keep only what the generated path needs:
   the rig, clips, weapons, holds, sockets, gloves, and the slimmed body as the
   weight source.
4. Add a Chinese army look (PLA) and a French army look. Point the Chinese
   FN-6 team (today Russian EMR) and the French Akeron team (today German
   Flecktarn) and any other mismatched soldiers at them. The lead's lean, open
   to your judgement: a vehicle's crew wears its vehicle's army (French VBCI and
   VBL crews French, a USMC vehicle's crew MARPAT), which changes
   `CREW_SOLDIER` from faction-keyed to army data each vehicle family names.
5. No backward compatibility and no migrations: hard cutovers. No shipped
   squad may mix generated and procedural soldiers, so a merge to `staging`
   happens only where every kit wearing an army's looks is fully converted
   (army by army, or all at once).
6. The load budget is arbitrary: retune it as needed; retire unused budget
   where there is a reason (e.g. print recipe textures the generated looks no
   longer use).
7. Cost reporting: log every generation call's charge; the closeout reports the
   cost per body and in total.
8. Human review checkpoints are non-blocking (open the shots, wait about 5
   minutes, then decide on the evidence and record it). The implementer judges
   output itself; every visual slice ends with an unprimed screenshot-critique,
   and compare-screenshots where there is a prior look or reference.
