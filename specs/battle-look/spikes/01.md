# Spike 01 verdict: renderer port feasibility

**Verdict: go.** The foundation ports on a pruned closure of 33 files and 3,697 lines, under the 4k kill line. No kill criterion tripped:
- The TypeGPU `~unstable` encoder works.
- Four receiver-fitted cascades cover 5 m to 1.6 km without acne or detached shadows, swimming within 2–3 texels.
- Overlays keep their colours exactly after post.
- Allocations return to baseline after resize and rebuild.

The port is not a copy, though. The shadow pillar has to be adapted, and one landmine changes slice 12's overlay seam (see "Landmines"). Measured 2026-09-25 against `~/dev/game` HEAD `d4394dfc`.

## What was built (throwaway)

A spike route, `/lab/spike-foundation`, draws the live village battle through the ported foundation behind our existing `BattleScene` seam. `BattleScene` is the interface `LabViewport` drives: `render`, `setWorld`, `setOverlay`, `setInstances`, `setFog`. The frame runs in five stages:

1. **Shadows.** Four cascades of 2048², one depth pass each. Casters are the world, the standing structures and the unit proxies.
2. **HDR world.** The sky background, then opaque and translucent world into rgba16float with 4× MSAA. Surfaces use the ported PBR, PMREM, sun shadow and aerial haze. Our sRGB vertex colours are the albedo. `FogTerm` (the 8 m bitset, bilinear, as today) applies before post.
3. **Post.** The ported bloom, grade and AgX, written into the canvas (`bgra8unorm`).
4. **Overlays.** Today's flat overlay shader draws into an MSAA rgba8 target, depth-tested against the world depth, then resolves.
5. **Composite.** One premultiplied blend over the canvas.

Its inputs:
- the 48-float camera;
- our `world_layout()` triangles, with no relief exaggeration;
- every allocation destroyed explicitly.

The prototype is kept for reference on the local branch `spike/01-renderer-port-proto` (commit `089efc2`, never merged). It contains:
- `throwaway/spike01/` (port copy, `spikeScene.ts`, closure tool);
- the wiring in `LabViewport.tsx`, `router.tsx`, `fixtures.json`, `battleOverlay.ts` and `scene.ts`;
- `web/scenes/spike-foundation.mjs`.

It never merges.

**`technique`** (rewritten from reading):
- `battle-renderer/src/world/frame.ts` (`4d0700704baa62da`) becomes our `BattleFrame`. The rewrite adds:
  - the overlay and structure split (landmine 2);
  - the overlay pass after post and its composite;
  - registry ownership;
  - timestamp markers.

  The source's frame owns the camera buffer and draws sky, world and post in one call. It has no overlay stage.
- `game-renderer/src/environment/environment.ts` (`8ab14baa77d01cd8`) is presets and types. It becomes `presentation.light` plus `summer.json`, read by the copied parameter modules. ## TypeGPU 0.12.5 `~unstable` encoder: works

- `root['~unstable'].createCommandEncoder()` encoded every spike frame: about 1,225 frames per camera per 10 s run, and every run.
- The PMREM bake uses it, including `setViewport` and `setScissorRect` on its typed passes.
- The typed shadow passes (`encoder.beginRenderPass`, then `pipeline.with(pass)`) interleave on the same encoder with raw passes via `root.unwrap(encoder)`. The raw passes are the sky, post, the overlay composite and the timestamp markers.
- There were no validation errors on the page.
- A render pipeline with **no fragment stage** also works in 0.12.5. So cascade casters need not use the source's `frag_depth` workaround, which disables early-Z.

1920×1080, production build, M5 Pro, hardware Metal, Chromium. 10 s per camera, redraw forced every frame, GPU lock held.

- Frame intervals stay pinned to the 120 Hz display, like row 0 (8.3 / 9.2 / 9.3). The foundation costs about 1.2–1.5 ms of GPU time and about 0.35 ms more CPU per frame. The CPU goes on the receiver-range ray grid, the cascade fits and TypeGPU encoding.
- GPU phase split, as a mean over 1,225 frames:
  - shadow 0.22–0.32 ms;
  - world 0.62–0.85 ms;

  - the MSAA rgba16float colour target (63 MiB);
  - four 2048² shadow layers (64 MiB);
  - the MSAA depth target (32 MiB);
  - the MSAA overlay target (32 MiB).

  Resolving the overlay into the HDR MSAA target is not possible, because the overlays must stay out of post. Slice 12 can drop the separate overlay MSAA target if AA on overlay lines is not needed.

## Overlays after post: colours unchanged

Two overlay-only frames are captured over the canvas, one cleared black and one white. Where they agree, the overlay is opaque. On those pixels (eroded 1 px, DOM hidden), the post-processed spike frame matches the overlay colour exactly: **0 of 24,946 pixels differ**. Nine cameras were captured, and four of them show opaque overlay interiors (WARNO, main default, village, orders); the thin order lines vanish under the erosion. It also matches the old flat renderer's pixel, apart from 960 pixels at the village camera. Those 960 pixels are units that moved between the two separately paused battles, not colour.

Composited after AgX, the overlays are neither regraded nor fogged.

## Allocation baseline

Measured at 1920×1080 → 1280×720 → 1920×1080 → `rebuild()`. Every count and byte figure is identical at each step: 59 buffers, 39,126,432 bytes, 23 textures.

The ported owners destroy what they create, because TypeGPU's `root.destroy()` does not. Two async rules follow:
- The frame's resize is async (post pipelines). So a scene must tolerate `render`, `setInstances` and friends after `dispose`, while `LabViewport.rebuild()` awaits the next scene.
- Targets must be swapped only after the new post chain exists.

## Landmines found (these change later slices)

1. **The source's cascades split from the camera near plane: two cascades to 1.5 km.** At our heights the whole map is 0.8–2.6 km from the eye, so they would spend their resolution on air. The splits must run over the map's view-depth range. The spike takes that range from a 13×13 screen-ray grid against the map box. The shader's receiver depth must use the same normalisation as the breaks. This is the one real design change in the shadow pillar; slice 13's "cascade coverage across the village distances" test should pin it.
2. **The overlay mesh is not all overlay.** `buildBattleOverlay` puts the **standing buildings** (`buildStandingStructures`, the fallible structures drawn apart from the world) into `overlay.opaque`. Composited after post, the village's buildings would come out:
   - flat-lit;
   - ungraded;
   - unfogged;
   - and would cast no shadows, because casters come from the world.

   The spike split them, via a throwaway `leadingStructureFloats` count, into the HDR world pass with fog off, and into the casters.

   Slice 12's seam must separate knowledge-drawn world geometry (standing structures, and probably remembered ruins and wrecks from `remainsLayer`) from display-space overlays. That could be `WorldMeshes` gaining a `structures` layer, or `setStructures()`. Otherwise "overlays after post" breaks the buildings.
3. **Per-pass GPU timing on Metal:** empty compute passes write no timestamps (all zero). The spike marks phases with 1×1 cleared render passes carrying `timestampWrites`, which work. Phase boundaries still overlap on the tile-based GPU. Slice 10's benchmark and slice 12's pass inspector should report the frame total and treat phases as indicative.
4. **Texture memory more than triples** (63 → 231 MiB at 1080p). No budget until slice 27, but the benchmark should report it.
5. **Below the horizon, the sky shows `GROUND_BOUNCE_TINT`**, a tan band, past the map edge at WARNO pitch (`spike-default.png`). This is cosmetic. Slice 13 or 16 needs a backdrop or horizon treatment.
6. **Bind groups need no manual renumbering.** Only the camera layout is pinned (`$idx(0)`). Environment, shadow and fog layouts are unindexed, and TypeGPU slots them, so landmine 12's "renumber per the manifest" reduces to "pin only the camera".

## Unprimed critique

A fresh sub-agent was given all ten spike frames and eight 2–4× crops, with a neutral defect-hunting prompt that included the fog-versus-shadow question. Its findings, triaged against the flat frames of the same scene:

**It confirms the spike's shadow claims (high confidence):**
- no acne or striping on any lit surface;
- a consistent sun direction across all frames;
- soldier-proxy, tank and building shadows touch their casters;
- nothing floats.

**Findings caused by the port, for slice 13 to act on:**
- **Shadows are a saturated teal, blue-black decal** (about `#2a5058` on grass), not darkened ground. The cause is the sky-only fill with a low environment intensity under a blue sky. It is exactly the shadow-versus-fog readability slice 15 depends on. Retune fill and grade.
- **Strategic and default views show the map as a plate in a khaki void**, with a yellow-olive cast and low contrast. This is landmine 5 (the below-horizon ground-bounce tint) plus exposure.
- **At eye height, the horizon is a hard line where the map edge meets the sky.** The aerial haze does not dissolve the edge.
- **The exposure is flat and midtone.** Grass, fogged ground and buildings share a narrow value range.
- **Trunk shadows read as thin hard tick marks** at 0.3–0.6 m per texel. Slice 19's trees replace the poles; the PCF radius may need to scale per cascade.

**Pre-existing, the same in the flat frames, and out of this spike's scope:**
- The sawtooth road edges come from the terrain triangle classification (slice 16).
- Translucent forest boxes with bare poles, and the road showing through them (slice 19).
- 8 m fog stair-steps and soft fog discs; fog reading as cloud shadow at ground level; buildings and trunks staying lit inside fog (slices 14–15). The last is by design today: knowledge-drawn structures use fog off.
- Overlay styling: ribbon rings, the heavy selection ring, magenta order lines, facing wedges. These are proven pixel-identical to the old renderer.
- Monolithic buildings with no scale cues (slice 22).

**Inspected and not confirmed:**
- "Shadow clipped at the fog boundary" (swim-street). The same frame's geometry puts the shadow wedge on seen ground in front of a fogged building, and `spike-village.png` shows shadows continuing across fogged ground.
- The "doubled tank edge". It is the proxy's hull, turret and barrel boxes; there is no temporal AA in the frame.

## What slice 12 should do

Adopt the files above in their stated modes, as one `BattleFrame`:
- shadows (four cascades, receiver-fitted);
- HDR world: the sky, opaque world plus structures, translucent;
- post, then the display-space overlay pass and composite.

Also:
- the 48-float `CameraUniform` from `renderer-core/cameraUniform.ts`, filled from slice 09's `CameraController`;
- the `FogTerm` stub as the spike's `fogSeen` over the 8 m bitset, applied before post;
- the structure/overlay split from landmine 2;
- registry-owned targets with texture-byte telemetry;
- timestamp markers as in landmine 3.

Slice 13 then tunes `presentation.light`: sun, exposure, grade, bloom, cascades. In the spike, shadowed ground reads as a dark teal from sky-only fill, and sunlit albedo reads washed.
