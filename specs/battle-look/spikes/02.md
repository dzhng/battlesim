# Spike 02 verdict: sight-fog technique

**Chosen: candidate 1, sight lights.** Each own eye gets a polar horizon map, and the maps are culled per 16 px screen tile. It passes every item of the acceptance bar. **Rejected: candidate 2, the 1 m viewshed.** It fails edge sharpness by construction (5–7 px stair-steps at ground framing), and its update cost does not amortize (see "Why the viewshed lost").

Measured 2026-09-25 on this Mac: M5 Pro, hardware Metal, Chromium 148 headless, 1920×1080, DPR 1. The prototype was a standalone raw-WebGPU page over the simulation's own exported geometry and sight inputs, not the game renderer. It never merges; a copy is kept in `throwaway/spike02-prototype/` (see "Evidence").

| acceptance bar (provisional, from the slice) | sight lights, 4096 × 64 | 1 m viewshed |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 1 px of stair-stepping on building edges at ground framing | **0.5 px**; the edge sits ≤ 1.4 px from the true corner line | 5–7 px; 12–14 px off |
| about 2 ms or less per frame, amortized, at 100 a side | **0.5–1.6 ms** typical; 2.2 ms if every eye moves at every 30 Hz publication | 1.0–1.3 ms at a 5 Hz update; 5.7–6.1 ms at 30 Hz |
| ≤ 5% of cells disagreeing with the simulation, outside a one-cell band | **0.02–0.60 %** | 0.01–0.60 % |

## The seam slice 14 builds (contract first)

**Inputs, per frame, from the observation:** one row per own eye:
- `eye` (x, y, z);
- base range;
- `forward` bearing;
- `shape {front, side, rear}`.

That is exactly slice 04's `OwnUnit.sight {eye[], forward, shape, range}`. A garrisoned squad gives one row per occupied slot, as `sensing::eyes` does.

**Inputs, static:**
- The terrain height grid, sampled with the simulation's triangle rule (south-west to north-east diagonal).
- The occluding props (oriented boxes, `PropKind::occludes`) and the forest rects with canopy heights.
- Rule numbers: `fog_target_height_m`, `forest_attenuation_m`, `forest_full_block_m`.

Known props update the occluder list the way `known_props` does today.

**GPU resources:**
- **One horizon map per eye:** 4096 azimuth rays × 64 log-spaced radial bins (1 m to the eye's range), one `u32` per bin, packed as:
  - an f16 horizon slope: the steepest occluder slope up to the bin's far edge;
  - a u8 jump position: where inside the bin the horizon jumped;
  - a u8 foliage length: metres of foliage so far, in 0.5 m steps.
- **One coarse terrain map per eye:** 512 rays × 64 bins, same packing.
- **Per-tile eye lists:** 16×16 px tiles; the prototype used 8 MiB (`u32` indices, 256 per tile). `u16` indices with 128 per tile would take 2 MiB. The measured maximum was 72 eyes in a tile.

**Passes:**
1. **Update, only for eyes whose position changed since the last publication.**
   - March terrain and foliage at 512 rays. Steps start at 0.5 m and grow as 1% of distance, capped at 4 m.
   - Merge at 4096 rays: intersect each ray analytically with the occluding boxes (entry distance and top slope), then take the running maximum with the interpolated terrain map.
   - The sight shape is applied per fragment, so **turret traverse or a new bearing never rebuilds a map**. Only movement does.
2. **Per frame:**
   - The depth of the frame.
   - A compute pass bounds each tile's world xy from depth and lists the eyes whose range circle touches it.
   - `FogTerm` tests the fragment against its tile's eyes and stops at the first eye that sees it.

**`FogTerm(world position, surface normal, pixel, is_ground) -> seen`** is the shared material function (one owner, as the README's table says). Slice 12's stub must take these four arguments, not a world position alone:
- **Ground probes at the simulation's target height** (+1 m), so displayed fog means what `ground_visibility` means.
- **Prop and unit faces probe 0.1 m outside the face**, along the normal.
- **Only eyes in front of a face count**: `dot(n, eye − p) > 0`. See landmines 2–3.

**Per eye in the tile list:**
1. Range: base range × `shape(θ − forward)`, linear in angle from front to side (90°) to rear (180°). Slice 04 owns the real shape; the spike's was hand-written.
2. Pick the radial bin that contains the fragment's distance.
3. Blend the two neighbouring azimuth rays (landmine 4).
4. Before the bin's jump position, use the previous bin's horizon; after it, this bin's.
5. Interpolate foliage linearly inside the bin (landmine 5).
6. Seen when `(p.z − eye.z) / d ≥ horizon`, and `d ≤ range·exp(−foliage / attenuation)`, and foliage is below the full block.

## Measurements

### Edge sharpness at Broken Arrow ground framing

The camera sits 10–12 m up, pitched 20–25° down, with a 40° vertical field of view: about 2–4 cm per pixel on the ground. The test edge is the street recon's sight-shadow edge past building B's corner, about 150 m from the eye. It was measured at two framings: across the screen ("far"), and at the corner ("near").

Two metrics:
- **Stair**: the largest deviation from a straight chord over a 64 px window, which is the stair-stepping.
- **Position**: the error against the true line through the eye and the corner, projected analytically.

| technique | stair, far / near | position error max (mean), far | near |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A, 4096 × 64 (chosen)** | **0.5 / 0.5 px** | **1.4 (0.8) px** | **1.3 (0.7) px** |
| A, 2048 × 64 | 0.5 / 0.5 px | 5.7 (4.1) px | 4.3 (2.9) px |
| A, 1024 × 64 | 0.5 / 0.5 px | 8.3 (6.0) px | 6.0 (4.2) px |
| B, 1 m viewshed, bilinear, threshold 0.5 | **5 / 7 px** | 12.3 (5.2) px | 13.7 (6.3) px |

Sight-light edges never stair-step: a building's sight-shadow side is a ray from the eye. Resolution only moves the edge sideways, by up to one azimuth bin. 4096 rays put it within about 1.5 px of the corner that casts it. The 1 m viewshed's edge is a staircase of texels, about 20 px per step at this framing. That fails the one-pixel bar by construction, and finer texels only move the problem one zoom level in.

### Agreement with the simulation

GPU fog was evaluated at every 8 m cell centre at the simulation's target height. It was compared with `ground_visibility` outside a one-cell band around the simulation's own boundary:
- **Isotropic**: today's rule.
- **Directional**: against a CPU copy of `visibility::sweep` that shrinks each ray's range by the shape, which slice 04 will do. This copy reproduces the simulation's field bit for bit in the isotropic case.

| scene (eyes) | A isotropic | A directional | B isotropic | A inside forests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| village at start (8) | 0.02 % | 0.02 % | 0.05 % | 0 % |
| village street, blue moved in (8) | 0.60 % | 0.60 % | 0.60 % | 0 % |
| endurance minute 3, blue (96) | 0.04 % | 0.04 % | 0.01 % | 0.34 % |
| endurance vehicles only, directional (24) | — | 0.01 % | 0.01 % | 0 % |

The bar is 5%. What remains is the simulation's quantization, not the GPU's:
- It marks cells by 8 m ray samples, which leaves one-cell holes.
- It occludes with 8 m cell-top boxes. A building's silhouette is therefore shifted by up to a cell, and long sight-shadow edges drift outside the band. This is the street's 0.6%.

The disagreement map: `throwaway/evidence/spike02/diff-street.png`.

### Foliage and canopy

Foliage accumulates along each ray while the sight line is below the canopy, which is the sweep's rule. It is stored per bin and interpolated linearly inside the bin. Disagreement inside forest cells is 0–0.34%. Tree crowns are surfaces, not occluders, as in the simulation. They take the face probe and the facing test like any prop.

### GPU cost at 1920×1080 (final code)

Method:
- 150 interleaved rounds of three frames (no fog, A, B) on a busy GPU.
- Timestamp queries with quantization disabled.
- Medians; the p95 was within 10% throughout.
- "Fog" is the A frame's cull plus main pass, minus the no-fog frame's main pass.
- These ran under the GPU lock, with one CPU core busy with another agent's `endurance_report`.
- An earlier quiet run of the same code, before the foliage fix, measured 5–30% lower.
- A run while `village_report` saturated the CPU measured 1.5–1.8× higher.

| scene (eyes) | A fog per frame: ground / default / strategic | A full rebuild (terrain + merge) | B fog per frame | B full re-sweep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| village street (8) | 0.18 / 0.26 / 0.59 ms | 0.44 ms (0.36 + 0.08) | 0.00–0.03 ms | 0.76 ms |
| endurance (96) | 1.02 / 0.49 / 0.28 ms | 2.42 ms (1.43 + 0.99), 0.025 ms per eye | 0.00–0.38 ms | 11.4 ms (8.4 quiet) |
| stress, both sides (193) | 1.10 / 0.45 / 0.41 ms | 4.72 ms | 0.00–0.47 ms | 23.5 ms |

A needs the frame's depth before the colour pass for the tile cull. The prototype's depth prepass cost 0.11–0.37 ms on the proxy scene, and that is not included above (landmine 6).

**Amortized at 100 a side (endurance, 60 fps):**

| case | per frame |
|---|---|
| Worst: every eye moved at every 30 Hz publication | 1.21 ms of rebuild + 0.28–1.02 ms of fog = **1.5–2.2 ms** |
| Half the eyes moving | **0.9–1.6 ms** |
| Rebuilding at the sweep's own cadence (every 6 ticks, 5 Hz) | **0.5–1.2 ms** |

That is within the "about 2 ms" bar, marginal only in the all-moving worst case. A per-frame rebuild budget, round-robin, caps it. The viewshed has to re-sweep every eye whenever any eye moves, because its texture is a union: 11.4 ms per update, or 5.7 ms per frame at 30 Hz.

Memory is sight lights' real cost. 2048 rays halves it, but the edge then sits about 5 px off the corner at ground framing. A tiered option: 4096 rays for eyes within about 150 m of the camera, 1024 for the rest.

## Why the viewshed lost

- **Sharpness fails by construction.** A 1 m texel is 25–50 px at ground framing. Bilinear filtering plus a threshold gives rounded 5–7 px stairs; no threshold gives a 20 px smear. Beating it would take a sub-metre texture over a 1.6–3 km map, which moves the cost into memory and sweep time.
- **Updates don't amortize.** The union must be re-swept whole: 11.4 ms per update at 96 eyes, against 2.4 ms for all 96 horizon maps.
- **It knows nothing about walls or roofs.** It stores one value per ground texel. A facade has to sample 0.65 m out in front of itself, and a facing test is impossible.

It does agree with the simulation best, because it is the same algorithm at 1 m. And it is nearly free per frame. It stays the right tool for coarse, sim-side needs, not for pixels.

## Landmines (each one was hit and fixed in the prototype; slice 14 must build the fix in)

1. **A half-bin radial lag lights roofs.** A lookup that lags one radial bin, to avoid self-occlusion, lit a strip of every roof. Fix: store *where* in the bin the horizon jumped (8 bits), and switch from the previous bin's value to this bin's at that point, plus a tenth of a metre.
2. **Probing above a surface lights roofs from afar.** A point 5 cm above a roof is truly visible across a band t₀ × 0.05 / (roof − eye) wide: 6 m at 800 m. Probing inside the surface fails the other way: the wall's own box occludes it. Fix: probe 0.1 m outside along the normal, and count only eyes in front of the face. Roofs above every eye are then never seen, which is correct (see "Changes to slices 12, 14, 15").
3. **Knowledge-drawn structures are fog-exempt today** (spike 01's landmine 2 draws them fog-off). With sight lights they must take `FogTerm` with their normals, or their faces never show sight shadows.
4. **Grazing facades sawtooth.** At a 3° grazing angle, a facade's jump distance moves about 22 m between adjacent rays. Choosing before/after per ray and then blending produced teeth. Fix:
   - Interpolate the jump distance across the two rays, then choose once.
   - Do this when the two rays' jumps agree within `1 m + 1% d + 1.5·d·Δθ·tan(incidence)`, where the incidence comes from the fragment normal.
   - Otherwise (a silhouette corner), choose per ray and blend.
5. **Stepping foliage with the horizon undercounts forest.** Foliage stepped with the horizon's before/after choice undercounted forest by up to a bin, about 60 m at 600 m. That produced 7% disagreement inside forests. Fix: foliage is continuous, so interpolate it linearly inside the bin.
6. **The tile cull needs depth before colour.** Spike 01's frame has no depth prepass and draws the world at 4× MSAA. Slice 12 must provide the frame's depth first: a depth-only prepass, or a Hi-Z from the previous frame. The cull reads one sample per pixel.
7. **The terrain march dominates the build.** A full 4096-ray march cost 8.5 ms at 96 eyes. Marching terrain at 512 rays and intersecting props analytically at 4096 costs 2.4 ms, with identical agreement and edge metrics. Terrain is smooth; building corners are not.
8. **GPU timing on a shared machine.** Submit-and-wait timing let the GPU clock down, and 0.26–1.34 ms swings in the same pass swamped the fog deltas. Interleaved, back-to-back batches fixed it. Contention from other agents still moved absolute numbers 1.5–1.8×, so slice 14 should measure the fog delta paired, not absolutely.

## What slice 14 must build

- `FogVisibility` in `battle-renderer`:
  - the per-eye maps and coarse terrain maps above, with lifetimes in the resource registry;
  - rebuilds for moved eyes only, capped per frame;
  - the tile cull compute pass;
  - the lookup as one shared WGSL function called by `FogTerm`.
- **Oracle vectors for the lookup.** A CPU implementation of this exact lookup, the "Rust oracle" of slice 14's contract. The prototype's CPU reference sweep, a copy of `visibility::sweep` with per-ray range, is the other half of the agreement test.
- **Fixture numbers**, provisional, in `presentation.fog_geometry`:
  - `azimuth_bins` 4096;
  - `terrain_azimuth_bins` 512;
  - `radial_bins` 64;
  - `tile_px` 16;
  - `face_probe_m` 0.1.
- **The scene assertion:** disagreement at 8 m cell centres, outside the one-cell band, at most 5%. Expect about 0.6% on the street.
- **The edge test:** a ground-framing capture of one eye past a building corner, with stair ≤ 1 px and position ≤ 2 px against the analytic line.

## Changes to slices 12, 14 and 15

- **12:**
  - `FogTerm` takes the world position, surface normal, pixel coordinate and a ground flag. The 8 m bitset stub ignores the last three.
  - The frame provides depth before the world colour pass (landmine 6).
  - Structures are drawn with fog on (landmine 3).
- **14:** as above. The acceleration structure, delegated to it, is settled here: tile lists from depth bounds.
- **15:**
  - True per-pixel sight leaves every face above every eye unseen, so roofs and tree canopies read as fogged even inside a fully seen village. Whether a roof inherits its building's seen facades, and a canopy the ground under it, is a look decision for slice 15, and possibly the user's. This spike rendered them as sight says.
  - Sight shadows cast from buildings run beside the same buildings' long sun shadows at 16:00. Dimming and cooling did not keep them apart in the critique (see "Critique"). The unseen style needs a cue that sun shadow never has, and shadow inside fog must stay distinguishable from fog.

### Critique

A fresh sub-agent, with no backstory, saw both full sets and ten zoomed crops, labelled `set1` (A) and `set2` (B) (`critique/`). It was asked the fog-versus-shadow question. **It judged set1 less wrong at the boundary**: straight edges starting at the building corners, no sawtooth, no speckle, no offset at the corner.

Its set2 findings confirm the rejection: stair-steps and teeth, detached speckle along every edge, the edge meeting the wall about 23 px right of the corner, and scalloped fog on walls. Its set1 findings, triaged:

- **"A lit diagonal band across a fogged wall"** (`village-start`). This is correct geometry. The 20 m ridge, 520 m from the recon, hides building A's west wall below 3.4–4.7 m, depending on where along the wall the ray crosses the crest. `probe-A-split1.png` shows it close up. It is a readability question for slice 15, not a technique fault.
- **"Tree canopies and roofs in seen ground render fogged."** This is true under per-pixel sight: faces above every eye are never seen. It is the slice 15 decision in "Changes to slices 12, 14 and 15"; B hides it only because a ground texel stands in for the canopy.
- **"About 1 px stair-steps at zoom"** (aliasing). This is within the bar. It is slice 14's edge filtering (its feedback rule): evaluate per MSAA sample, or ramp over one pixel.
- **"Rectangular notches at the far ends of the endurance wedges."** This is true union geometry: where one eye's range ends, another eye's edge takes over. The notches are unchanged with directional sight off and with the radial bins doubled.
- **"Fog reads as long low-sun shadow"** (endurance, street-all), and sun shadows inside fog merge with it. This affects both sets: it is the unseen look, not the geometry, and it is slice 15's core risk. At a 16:00 sun, sight shadows cast from buildings run beside sun shadows cast from the same buildings. Dimming and cooling alone did not separate them in this prototype.

The Preview checkpoint was not opened: this is a spike, and the coordinator relays the frames.
