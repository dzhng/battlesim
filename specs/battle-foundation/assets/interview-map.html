A battlefield built around information

Browser combined-arms combat inspired by WARNO and Broken Arrow. Physical projectiles, readable information, and deliberate positioning; less routine management. This is the decision map, not an implementation or a claim that the game has been built.

1 · Known knowns

The repository initially contained skill configuration, with no game implementation. The requested reusable foundation lives in the sibling ../game. The original brief calls for infantry squads, tanks, vehicles and transports, helicopters, jets, radar AA, artillery, and supplies. Extensive unit catalogs and deck building are deferred.

Combat roles from the brief

Infantry is fragile when exposed or suppressed but survives through concealment, cover, and disengagement. Tanks combine long reach and armor with weak optics, needing reconnaissance and protection from scarce anti-tank weapons. Specialist infantry trades squad strength and ammunition for strong counters. Vehicles trade protection for mobility; helicopters favor quick strikes over prolonged exposure. Jets are powerful, delayed strikes checked by long-range radar AA. Exact lethality is balance intent, not a finalized damage table.

Projectiles, armor, and suppression · 14 decisions
IDDecision · owner: userWhy
P01All weapons, including bullets, use physical projectiles. Shot deviation represents accuracy; travel and collision determine direct hits.Enable dodging and emergent interactions.
P02Flight includes gravity; aiming compensates with elevation. Weapons lead using observed velocity and flight time, never future orders or hidden motion.Make predictable motion hittable and evasive changes useful.
P03Direct-fire weapons use normal low ballistic arcs; only dedicated indirect-fire weapons choose high arcs. Check the actual curved firing path.Preserve artillery roles while honoring gravity.
P04Unguided rockets require no post-launch guidance; changing target motion can evade them.Distinguish unguided flight from seeker guidance.
P05ATGMs require stationary launchers and the launcher’s own visual identification to sustain guidance; shared identification cannot sustain it.Breaking launcher sightlines has tactical value.
P06Moving or canceling guidance releases the crew immediately. Lost guidance never reacquires; the missile continues toward its last target point. A target that stays there can still take normal damage.Loss of tracking creates a chance to evade, not automatic immunity.
P07Radar AA missiles likewise require a stationary launcher and its own radar contact; lost contact ends guidance permanently and leaves flight toward the last target point.Reuse the support-guided interaction.
P08Infantry anti-air missiles acquire before launch, then track with their own seeker. The launcher may move immediately; terrain breaking seeker sight ends tracking.Give mobile infantry AA a distinct role.
P09Physical collisions and blast apply regardless of allegiance or intended target. A projectile is consumed at its first damaging hit; it does not overpenetrate into another unit.Support collateral damage without through-and-through simulation.
P10Vehicles have front, side, rear, and roof armor. Penetration determines whether a hit defeats armor; penetrating hits damage vehicle health. No internal component simulation.Make flanking matter with manageable complexity.
P11Weapons withhold visibly unsafe shots when friendly vehicles obstruct the predicted path or occupy the blast danger area. Friendly infantry does not trigger this prefire check, but can still be hit.Avoid knowingly firing through vehicles without infantry constantly blocking fire.
P12Occupied-building protection is an incoming accuracy penalty, not an additional wall test or damage reduction against occupants. Buildings still block shots through them toward targets beyond.Treat misses as rounds stopped by walls, as requested.
P13Blast exposure is resolved per soldier using radius and distance; cover lowers damaging-fragment hit probability. Successful hits retain normal damage for that distance.Extend probability-based cover to explosions.
P14Near misses, impacts, and explosions add weapon-specific suppression even without damage. Suppression reduces infantry movement and fire rate, never forces retreat, and decays after a lull.Enable pinning and flanking while retaining player control.

Corrections that must not be lost

  • Aim is per weapon, not shared per target across a unit.
  • One stationary flag covers aim, fire, and reload. Movement resets even completed aim for those weapons.
  • Visibility loss has a 1.5-second aim grace, with progress continuing; it does not preserve launcher missile guidance.
  • Lost launcher guidance flies toward the last target point, not merely along the instantaneous heading.
  • Supply vehicles can replace infantry casualties in surviving squads.
  • Hold fire means return fire only; there is no agreed absolute-silence policy.
  • Stop permits automatic combat to restart. Attack and attack-move switch the entire unit to fire at will.
  • Infantry does not trigger friendly-fire withholding, but remains physically hittable.
  • Slopes slow movement but share one passability cutoff across ground units.
  • Deployment is reversible and packing duration equals setup duration.
  • Automatic speculative exchanges remain unrestricted for the first version.

4 · Unknown unknowns — landmine cards

The targeted stage-4 sweep inspected relevant sections of 11 source files: terrain, missiles, pathfinding, simulation exports and timestep, publication producer/layout, GPU capabilities/device creation, battle scene composition, and the GPU terrain layer. Earlier scans also read manifests, architecture/authority docs, WASM exports, and renderer documentation. This was read-only source inspection, not runtime verification or a whole-repository audit. Exact future implementation files are not yet selected; O14 requires slice-level review.

R01 · Required architectural change

Terrain elevation is not gameplay geometry in the sibling.

Territory evidence: crates/sim/src/terrain.rs:25. Height is explicitly presentation/seating-only. Reusing it unchanged would disagree with terrain-blocked sight, slopes, and projectile impacts.

Consequence: Give simulation terrain and obstacle geometry authority; rendering must represent that same geometry. Details remain O02.

R02 · Required architectural change

The current projectile loop checks hits at ground landing.

Territory evidence: crates/sim/src/missiles.rs:293. The inspected fly_and_land loop waits for z ≤ 0 before ground hit resolution. That is unsuitable for bullets striking vehicles or aircraft, or terrain along the trajectory.

Consequence: Use path collision against terrain and moving bodies, with deterministic earliest-hit ordering. Do not substitute the old arrow system unchanged.

R03 · Required architectural change

Pathfinding ignores speed and permits outside-map ground.

Territory evidence: crates/sim/src/path.rs:1. The existing route planner avoids impassables rather than minimizing travel time; off-map passability could undermine water and chokepoints.

Consequence: Separate shortest-distance and fastest-time route policies; define map bounds and dynamic obstacle invalidation.

R04 · Architectural constraint

The publication seam exposes raw simulation records.

Territory evidence: web/src/battle/sim/publicationLayout.ts:37. Raw positions, health, and unit records are not a player observation boundary. Hiding models in the renderer alone leaves targeting, audio, overlays, and future clients able to learn hidden truth.

Consequence: Introduce side-specific observation/contact outputs while keeping full truth in the authority. A local process boundary is not multiplayer security.

R05 · Reuse sharp edge

The renderer has reusable composition, but cross-package dependencies.

Territory evidence: packages/battle-renderer/src/battleScene.ts:1. The scene imports environment, terrain, crowd, asset, and readout owners. Copying one entry file is not a standalone 3D engine port.

Consequence: Audit and port the chosen dependency slice. Keep unrelated campaign and old combat mechanics out of the new gameplay contracts.

R06 · OPEN: runtime measurement

Device support and buffer capacity are explicitly runtime-dependent.

Territory evidence: packages/renderer-core/src/device.ts:43. The device request requires WebGPU and asks for adapter-specific limits. Source reuse alone establishes neither compatibility nor the current machine’s frame budget.

Consequence: Verify the actual adapter/browser and measured workload before promising performance; see O11 and O13.

R07 · OPEN: O13

Permanent remains and continuous fire accumulate work.

Evidence: user decisions M06, W09, and P01. A 60-minute battle with reinforcement turnover is not bounded by the initial unit count.

Separate inactive remains from active simulation and use spatial/camera culling without deleting promised objects. Measure late-battle memory, collision, audio, and publication load.

R08 · DECIDED: retain initially

Speculative fire can sustain a feedback loop.

Evidence: V10 reveals every shot; W10 permits approximate-contact fire; W09 supplies unlimited default guns.

User explicitly chose unrestricted behavior for simplicity. Return fire only may also perpetuate a qualified exchange; do not claim it always silences one. Keep this as a playtest observation, not an unapproved burst cap.

R09 · Sharp edge; O02/O03

Uncertainty can accidentally become exact tracking.

Evidence: contacts are approximate but refreshed by firing, and guided weapons use their own sensors.

Do not continually center a visible marker on an unseen true position or let audio/tracers/automatic target cost expose forbidden truth. Define observation payloads and stable uncertainty rules before implementation.

R10 · Sharp edge; O02

Garrison cover is an intentional exception to naive mesh collision.

Evidence: P12 uses miss probability for walls protecting occupants, while M07 and P01 use physical obstacles.

A generic wall collider could accidentally make occupants immune or double-protected. Define how occupant hit regions and building hits coexist before coding; preserve the user’s abstraction.

R11 · OPEN: O07

An indestructible bridge can still become permanently blocked.

Evidence: persistent obstructing wrecks, indestructible bridges, and no selected wreck-clearing mechanic.

Review map redundancy or explicitly accept isolated forces. Do not quietly add despawning, wreck pushing, or engineering units.

R12 · Sharp edge; O04/O05

State transitions cross several independent systems.

Evidence: reversible deployment, stationary weapons, concurrent aim/reload, guidance support, visibility grace, Stop, and queued actions.

Create an explicit transition table before implementation. Use deterministic simulation-time events; animations and UI reflect state rather than controlling it.

First-encounter review checklist — proposed, not measured

  • The same setup and seed can be reset to compare tactical choices.
  • Scouting reveals enough information to improve an assault; concealed enemies do not leak exact positions.
  • Near misses suppress defenders and a coordinated flank succeeds more reliably than unsupported tanks.
  • A prompt retreat can rescue an ordinary ambush, while a strong ambush can still inflict losses.
  • Projectiles, collision, and visible terrain agree; friendly-fire consequences are understandable.
  • Supply deployment, finite stock, repair, ammunition, and infantry replacement support rotating survivors.
  • The interface explains blocked fire/movement and concurrent weapon readiness.
  • Performance on the user’s actual machine is measured, including a stressed late-battle state.
