# Implementation decisions and state transitions

These are **spec-authored resolutions** of interview OPEN items. They are concrete initial choices, not additional user answers. Preserve the user rules in `requirements.md`; reversible tuning freedoms are named below. If a proposed contract proves untenable, update this spec and the decision log before broadening code.

## Geometry and routing

Use a 4 m grid of height samples, triangulated consistently with a southwest-to-northeast diagonal. Height queries interpolate the same triangles that render; do not combine a bilinear collision surface with a differently triangulated visual surface. Prop collision is oriented boxes/convex prisms; soldiers use upright capsules; ground vehicles use oriented boxes and planar footprint avoidance. Bridges supply an explicit traversable top surface over impassable water. Ground bounds are closed. The grid acceleration is an implementation detail, not permission to round a bullet hit to a cell.

Use a shared 35° slope cutoff; reduce speed continuously up to it. Initial slope multiplier is `max(0.35, 1 - slopeDegrees/50)` below cutoff. Forest multipliers are in the fixture config, with roads taking precedence. Shortest routes minimize distance; fast routes minimize travel time using each unit's speed table. Account for footprint clearance for vehicles, shared terrain-access rules, and corner cutting. Dynamic obstacles invalidate affected paths. Replan only on order, relevant observed obstacle revision, or detected failure to advance; never every frame. A blocked unit waits with a reason, retains the destination, and retries on relevant change or new order, not an unbounded tight loop.

Static scene is known. Wrecks stop vehicles only; infantry cross them (what each prop kind blocks is one per-mover-class table — choices.md "What each kind of prop blocks"). Unknown wrecks cannot secretly change a distant player's route preview (ruins are the one exception: every side plans round a ruin, which stands exactly on the building it replaced — choices.md); navigation discovers them through legal observation or physical approach (architecture.md). Wrecks remain collision props, corpses remain visual records with no movement blocking. Infantry can use gaps too small for a vehicle because of body size, not a different slope rule. Ruins block all ground movement. Use two independent village approaches; permanent isolation from wrecks is permitted in later maps only if explicitly reviewed. No wreck clearing, despawn or bridge destruction is added.

## Sensors and knowledge

LOS casts go from observer eye to target visibility samples: a vehicle hull center/top and living soldier positions. An observer identifies a squad only when the configured fraction of its living soldiers qualifies; publication includes only observed member poses. The side shares qualifying sightings, without pooling subthreshold glimpses from separate observers. Team-shared identification carries class/cost and observed position/velocity, not enemy HP, ammo, orders or timers. Own-unit HIDDEN requires the configured concealed fraction to be exceeded and neither a currently identified enemy observer spotting it nor a known attacker engaging it. Unseen observers do not disclose themselves through this flag. Engagement uses the existing return-fire attacker memory, including unidentified firing contacts, and ends when that memory expires. Stale observer tracks within acquisition grace do not suppress HIDDEN. Forest concealment is a binary bonus on uncleared forest ground. Crown coverage attenuates sight through trees, independently of that bonus. The stronger of forest and garrison concealment wins. [Sensing](../../crates/sim/src/sensing.rs) owns these decisions; [the rules fixture](../../fixtures/game.json) owns their numbers.

An approximate contact uses a side-local ID unrelated to enemy IDs, layer, reported center/radius, evidence time and expiry. Each side holds at most one report per hidden enemy unit. No hidden class, velocity, cost, HP or exact location appears; a previously observed type may remain remembered. A firing episode samples a seeded offset inside the uncertainty radius once, then refreshes expiry without independently jittering every shot. Retain the reported center while the emitter stays inside its reported area; a fresh shot outside may update that one report's approximate area. Do not move a contact because its hidden source moves without fresh evidence. Last-known contacts center on the last observed position and expire after the configured lifetime. Identification immediately removes the related live report; internal linkage is not exported. Every contact marker has a white outline and an information label, including unknown firing reports. Live reports remain fully visible. On every removal, marker and label share a three-second visual fade and cannot be picked or targeted; renewed evidence replaces that same visual owner without a second patch. Contact acquisition targets its stored area; sample a point within that area when firing. Removed targeting permission must not revive when its opaque report slot is reused. Repeated speculative shots never receive hidden aim corrections. This one-report, consistent-presentation and removal-fade amendment is user direction from 2026-10-02; implementation status lives in [06](slices/06-contacts-and-audio.md).

Firing disclosure applies map-wide regardless of LOS, including fire at empty ground. Sound is separate: friendly listeners qualify by configured hearing radius, emit one merged cue per source/category/time bucket, quantized to eight direction sectors and near/far range bands. Rendering uses that cue, not the raw source coordinate; moving the camera cannot discover new sounds. Each living unit emits a category-specific base sound, including while idle; movement and firing alter its character/intensity, but only eligible friendly hearing yields player cues. Placeholder audio is synthesized locally and can be replaced later. Visible sound captions provide an accessibility alternative without stronger information. Projectiles are clipped to currently observable flight segments; hidden muzzle flashes/lights/trails and offscreen world debug tools cannot disclose true launch points beyond permitted contact evidence.

Physical hostile attack evidence can grant Return fire permission even when the shot misses. Grant to the explicit attacked unit, or units physically damaged/suppressed by an otherwise untargeted hostile projectile; the authority emits an attacker-contact reference, not hidden identity. Permissions persist only while that identified/approximate contact episode exists. When no contacts change, hidden-state mutations must leave the player's payload and decision outputs identical.

## Target selection and readiness

Use one target-acquisition state per weapon mount. Infantry default-gun mounts represent synchronized squad weapons: ready fire emits one physical bullet per living participating soldier, each with its own origin/scatter; a specialist mount has one operator/origin. This is a prototype organization choice, not a new per-soldier command UI. Unit data determines mount participation; casualties reduce default-gun fire volume.

Selection order: explicit compatible target; otherwise highest-cost identified damageable target; otherwise, for an unlimited-ammo default gun only, the nearest identified invulnerable target (a weapon with finite ammunition never fires at what it cannot hurt); otherwise, only while no identified enemy the policy permits stands within the mount's reach, the nearest eligible approximate contact for general-purpose mounts. An automatic area lock is dropped once such an enemy is in reach. Apply unit engagement policy first. Ties use distance then stable observed ID. No AP speculative fallback when HE is empty: later general-purpose-only choice overrides the earlier brief suggestion. The default gun remains usable. A weapon holds its valid current target through its pending shot, then reevaluates while reloading. An invalid target can be replaced earlier. A player order replaces immediately. Identification loss alone does not make the current target replaceable during its 45-tick grace: retain that acquisition, continue aiming toward the last observation, and withhold automatic shots until identification returns or grace expires. After expiry, normal selection may acquire its approximate contact from zero. An explicit player attack on the approximate contact can deliberately replace the retained acquisition during grace; it does not later restore the abandoned aim. Test this with automatic targeting enabled, not only an isolated timer.

Timers use simulation time. `loaded: Option<AmmoKind>` is separate from total remaining ammunition per kind; totals include any loaded round and are debited only on launch. For the tank cannon choose AP for identified armored vehicles and HE for infantry/ground/contacts; if the preferred type is empty use another compatible identified-target round, but never AP against an approximate contact. At reload start fix the selected kind. Changing to another kind resets unfinished reload; changing a fully loaded kind unloads the old round without reducing its total, clears loaded state and requires a full reload of the new kind. No instant AP-to-HE conversion and no discarded/ejected round loss. A target change using the same kind does not reset reload. Reload does not debit ammo. Unlimited ammunition is an explicit enum, not a very large number. Aim/reload may overlap; turret bearing and deployment readiness are independent firing gates. A simple finite turret traverse rate applies; aim can advance while rotating, but cannot fire before bearing tolerance. Garrison default weapons have unobstructed outward arcs; hull weapons follow body bearing if defined that way.

| Event | Aim/acquisition | Reload/ammo | Guidance and order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same identified target remains | Completed aim persists | Reload advances independently | Current order persists |
| Identification lost | Aim continues toward last observation for 45 ticks; no unseen motion; on expiry clear acquisition | Continues if otherwise allowed | Existing supported missile loses support immediately; exact-target shot cannot use grace as live vision |
| Identification returns within grace | Preserve progress/completion; update observed point | Unchanged | Previously lost missile never reacquires |
| Weapon switches to approximate contact | Fresh aim on ContactId; use area sampling | Continues unless AmmoKind changes; then follow the change-round rule | Cannot reuse exact-target acquisition to fire precisely into fog |
| New target | Reset aim | Reload continues unless AmmoKind changes; then follow the change-round rule | Explicit order can interrupt target lock |
| Begin movement, stationary weapon | Reset progress and acquired flag | Reset unfinished reload; preserve loaded round | Release support; movement starts unless unit must pack |
| Begin movement, mobile weapon | Continue | Continue | Apply moving accuracy; release launcher support if any |
| Stop | Clear current aim once | Reset incomplete reload once; preserve loaded ammo | Clear queue/move/attack intent and release guidance; preserve policy; automatic actions may restart next tick |
| Suppression | Aim unchanged initially | Scale reload/cycle progress by the suppression tier's fixed penalty (none, suppressed, pinned), without resetting elapsed progress | Movement speed also reduced by the tier; never auto-retreat |
| Fire | Keep target acquisition | Debit a finite round, set unloaded, start cycle/reload | Create projectile and map-wide reveal evidence |

If visibility returns after grace, reacquire even the same unit. If target dies unseen, do not cancel player attack using hidden knowledge: continue to last reported area until observation expires and destination is reached. A dead visible target completes the attack. Approximate-contact order remains at its point until expiration or explicit replacement. Automatic targeting never pursues. Attack-move halts only for targets it can presently engage (not infinite pursuit or an unarmed incompatible target).

## Flight and collision

30 Hz is the strategic tick, not a maximum projectile travel resolution. Gravity uses 9.81 m/s². Between acceleration changes integrate position with `p + v*dt + 0.5*a*dt²`, then velocity. Curved segments subdivide until chord error is at most 0.02 m, with a declared maximum 32 subsegments per tick; invalid authoring that exceeds this bound fails fixture validation rather than silently skipping contacts. For each segment sweep against terrain triangles and moving colliders using relative motion over the same interval; turning vehicles require conservative bounds and narrowed time-of-impact checks. Resolve earliest impact, with stable collider-ID tie breaking. A 900 m/s bullet must not tunnel through a 0.3 m target. Ballistic intercept solves gravity and observed constant velocity with bounded root search over the weapon lifetime; if no valid solution exists, report no firing solution. Low/high solutions are selected by weapon trajectory capability, never by secretly ignoring a ridge.

Build only the needed shape queries initially; do not introduce full rigid-body dynamics. The reference-query reproduction spike in slice 07 checks analytic results and documents the narrow chosen algorithm. Internal choice of acceleration tree/grid is delegated after measurement; replacing physical flight with hit rolls is not.

Accuracy chooses spread once at launch. Use independent zero-mean Gaussian deviations in the two axes perpendicular to the intended firing direction, truncated at ±3 standard deviations; `scatter_mrad` is the one-axis angular standard deviation in milliradians. No unguided row is tighter at its own `range_m` than `physics.min_spread_at_max_range_m` (the accuracy ceiling, checked at load); a guided row steers out its spread and is exempt. Convert with tangent to an aim-plane displacement before solving launch; deterministic combat RNG supplies the samples. For an identified target, cover comes only from its observed exposed body/known terrain. For a contact or ground order, sample the aim point first, then use static/remembered terrain protection at that point, never the hidden victim's position or class. A collateral hit never retroactively resamples accuracy or cover. Moving fire multiplies angular spread by sqrt(2) initially to approximate halved hit probability against a small target; verify actual probabilities over paired trials rather than treating this as a universal exact 50% result. Cover similarly increases spread; it never reduces successful hit damage. Known-area targeting combines sampled area and weapon dispersion, not a roll followed by an exact teleporting hit.

Armor uses the impacted box face (roof/front/side/rear), fixed penetration and fixed penetrating damage initially. Failure to penetrate stops the projectile and causes zero vehicle HP damage. No ricochets, overpenetration, internal modules or armor-angle/range modifiers initially. A damaging hit consumes the projectile; explosive projectiles apply their configured blast once. All teams are eligible for impact/blast damage. Friendly-vehicle prefire checks use predicted mean curved trajectory plus vehicle-sized margin and nominal blast radius; they are not perfect guarantees against future movement or dispersion. Infantry is omitted from this withholding query only, not physical hit queries.

Blast uses distance falloff and per-soldier fragment exposure sampling, not thousands of individually simulated fragment objects. For distance r inside blast radius R, base fragment-hit probability is 1-(r/R)^2, multiplied by cover's exposure factor; conditional damage is configured blast damage times max(0,1-r/R). Outside R both are zero. Direct impact applies direct damage once, then excludes that same body from its projectile's blast damage to avoid double charging. Other soldiers/bodies still receive blast normally. Near-miss suppression contribution is configured value times max(0,1-distance/radius), accumulated and clamped to [0,1]. Other solid obstacles occlude blast samples; the occupied target building uses its cover exposure factor once rather than an additional shell block. Vehicles in blast get distance-based damage subject to the explosive's armor/penetration profile; friendly vehicles count equally. Near-miss suppression is based on closest traveled path distance and nearby impacts, not target labels. One projectile contributes at most one near-miss suppression event per squad per tick, preventing duplicate integration from subsegments.

## Guided flight

Village ATGM launch requires a currently shared identified target, the operator’s clear physical line of sight, and an acquired stationary weapon. Detection range and concealment affect spotting; they do not shorten a known target’s guidance reach. Terrain, opaque props and fully blocking foliage still obstruct acquisition and guidance. Support capacity is one active missile per launcher initially. A loaded next round can reload/aim concurrently, but cannot launch while that support slot is occupied. Loss of shared identification or the operator’s physical line of sight, move, Stop, death, or garrison/embark transition releases support. Reappearance never restores it.

While supported, steering follows the side’s observed target motion within configured turn limits. On loss, the flight owner fixes a point ahead on the missile’s current velocity, projected onto the terrain. It steers to that point without further target updates and never reacquires. A body on the continuation can still take normal damage; collision, range and lifetime remain the termination limits. [`sim::flight::Projectiles::release`](../../crates/sim/src/flight/mod.rs) owns this continuation. No hidden homing remains.

## Garrisons and destruction

A building takes one whole squad, within its soldier capacity; a second squad, or a squad too big, is refused with a reason, no automatic squad split (one squad per building: battle-look decisions, 2026-09-28). Entry requires proximity and a stationary timer. Exit uses a short stationary timer and deterministic free positions outside the perimeter. Loaded/garrisoned transitions suspend irrelevant movement/weapon actions.

To implement probability-based building cover without target-dependent wall transparency, place abstract occupant hit capsules at perimeter firing slots on the exterior side of the facade. They are small exposed regions; shots that miss them hit the building shell, and any shot toward a unit beyond still encounters the shell. Scatter applies cover once. All projectiles query the same colliders regardless of intended target; the building is not globally disabled for an occupant-targeted shot. Own outgoing origins lie just outside the facade. Each soldier requests a slot for one weapon, using the same weapon priority as active use. Target selection uses the normal engagement policy and physical assessment at prospective windows, so a missing current window cannot prevent requesting one. Idle held-fire troops watch legal threats; once attackers are known, return-fire permission still constrains their requests. Use the closest free such slot with stable-index ties, or trade with the squadmate at a facing slot who needs it less (a single-operator weapon before a squad weapon, independent of remaining participant count). A soldier who changes slots holds fire for `garrison.window_swap_s` and stays for `window_hold_s`. Slot relocation is a one-tick garrison abstraction, consumes no movement order, and updates the exposed collision point and interior published body pose together. Reserve capacity evenly around facades; if no facing slot can be had, that weapon waits rather than firing through its own shell. Observation rays originate only from currently occupied slots; do not grant a hidden extra 360° roof sensor. Initial occupancy is distributed evenly, and each squad's slot reassignment only uses its permitted observations. Verify targets on all four sides and at corners; another building still obstructs normally.

Published member body poses stand on the interior side of their occupied facade bay, including while exiting; both own and identified enemy publications use the garrison owner’s placement. Indoor bodies keep normal depth occlusion, with no through-wall x-ray cue: that cue makes occupants read as figures stuck on walls or standing on roofs. The squad’s panel and building selection circle identify its position. Combat continues to use the abstract exterior exposure point. Ordinary garrison casualties retain that interior body pose in their persistent death record, even when the squad releases the building; collapse still governs loss of floor support. Corpse-pose changes enter the digest while live combat retains the exposure geometry.

Building HP is reduced only by weapons with structural damage. Collapse computes passenger-style survival per occupant, creates a lower impassable ruin collider, and evacuates survivors to nearest reachable external legal slots, pinned (suppression at least the pinned tier's level). If a survivor has no legal escape slot within the configured local search, it dies rather than teleporting through unrelated solids. Preserve corpse records and ruin permanently. Footprint remains blocked to ground units; actual lower rubble shape governs sight/projectile blocking.

## Deployment and supplies

Progress `d` is in [0,1], with one duration T in both directions. Service/fire requiring deployment is enabled only at d=1. Move intent sets desired d=0; translate only at zero. Cancel movement/Stop while packing sets desired d=1; reverse from current d. A stopped supply unit auto-deploys; explicit Pack can keep it packed and stationary for planned transport. Stop during setup cancels weapon actions but does not invent a permanent half-packed state: deployment intent returns to the stationary default on the next tick. This resolves Stop versus reversible deployment explicitly.

Service recipients must be stationary and not firing during the tick. Taking fire does not block service. Serve every eligible recipient at once, paying completed items in ascending own UnitId (as built: choices.md "Every eligible unit is served at once"); within a unit replenish finite ammo, then restore vehicle HP or missing members. Debits are atomic and stock cannot be negative. Each finite ammunition kind refills only to its authored capacity (initial ammo in the prototype fixture); health and squad strength likewise stop at authored maxima. No resurrecting an eliminated squad/vehicle. Replacements receive new soldier IDs while old corpse records remain. Replacements do not duplicate specialist ammo; mount inventory belongs to the unit. Repairs do not remove wrecks. No repair/refill of the supply vehicle itself, and no stock regeneration. Eligibility needs radius only initially, not extra line-of-sight or road connectivity rules.

## Controls and feedback

Left click/drag select, Shift adds selection or queues an order, right click terrain moves, right click an identified enemy/contact attacks, double-right-click terrain fast-travels. Each right-click move receives a local gesture token retained for 350 ms. A second click within 6 CSS pixels sends an UpgradeMove(token, Fastest) command for that specific active or queued order, even if the first was applied on a previous tick; it never appends a second waypoint or replaces unrelated Shift-queued orders. If that order has already completed, the upgrade is an acknowledged no-op. Preserve both accepted events in replay. Tokens are scoped by side/unit and validated like other command references. Command keys have one owner, `CommandBindings` (`web/src/battle/input/commandBindings.ts`), bound by physical key position; camera input (held keys, wheel, middle-drag, screen edge) has another, renderer-core's `CameraController`, and no command takes a camera key. A visible command bar exposes every action and unit policy; keyboard is optional. Pick with the shared camera ray against the authoritative surface representation. Issue no commands while typing in controls. Queue per-unit immutable intents; group offsets persist through move waypoints.

Individual weapon rings show aim/reload concurrently with ammo number/∞; guidance is an icon, deployment separate. Show above selected/hovered units and recently acting units when legible; when zoomed out retain full details in the selected-unit panel rather than merging weapon state. Action reasons come from sim: no compatible target, outside range, blocked trajectory, guiding, moving stationary weapon, deploying, out of ammo, route blocked, capacity full. Do not expose hidden obstacle identity in a reason.

## Delegated freedoms

Internal file subdivision, deterministic acceleration structure choice within measured budgets, primitive mesh topology, non-semantic colors/glyphs, and browser layout details are delegated. Provisional balance values may be changed with paired-fixture evidence and a recorded rationale, except selected 1.5 s grace and user invariants. The fixture table is the sole numeric owner after implementation starts. Information disclosure, rule changes, scope deletion, compatibility, hidden aim corrections, projectile deletion to hit budgets, and network model are not delegated. Missing consequential choices must be added to the spec before implementation spreads.
