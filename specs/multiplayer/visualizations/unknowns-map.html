Multiplayer: rooms and Frontline. Walked 2026-11-14. Nothing here is built; every line is a decision or a finding for the spec to carry.

What is being built

Rooms. A player creates a room, public or private. Public rooms are listed for anyone to join; a private room is entered by its code. The room is the current skirmish lobby with several humans in it, on either side.

Frontline. One button drops a player into a long battle that is already running, on the side that is losing. Each seat is played by a human or by its own AI, so there is always a fight.

Settled by the code

  • Each seat has its own wallet, 30-unit cap and army; a side shares vision, objectives and score. Every seat earns the same income whoever holds it (crates/sim/src/skirmish.rs:221).
  • Every seat has an AI ready from the first tick, and the per-side planner re-reads who holds each seat within 5 seconds (crates/sim/src/skirmish_ai/team/mod.rs:200). Five AIs a side is a tested size.
  • The rules allow 10 seats a side; the lobby's cap is 5 (apps/battle-lab/src/skirmishSetup.ts:15).
  • The battle's owner in the browser takes its clock, timer and channel from its host, so Node can host it (web/src/battle/sim/authority.ts:42).
  • The sim is single-threaded: one battle, one core.
  • A running battle cannot be saved or restored. A joiner can only start from a per-side snapshot sent by a live battle.
  • There is no backend, database, account or player name today. The site is static files on Vercel.

  • One side's stream is about 2 KB a tick, roughly 60 KB/s. A join snapshot was 79 KB at the start and 0.6 to 0.9 MB after ten minutes.
  • Bento's machine: Linux, 8 cores (Xeon Platinum 8259CL), 16 GB, Node 24, no Rust toolchain.
  • Bento's private route passes a 30 Hz binary stream through unbuffered (one record per chunk, 33 ms apart), cuts it at 300 seconds, and cuts server-sent events at 240 seconds. An order's round trip from California was about 95 ms.
  • Bento's public route, which anonymous players use, treats streams the same way: unbuffered, cut at 300 and 240 seconds, cross-origin headers intact. An order's round trip there was about 300 ms from California, three times the private route's.
  • Bento apps do not support WebSockets. Convex cannot run a battle (1 second per function, 30 minutes per action).

Confirmed preferences and delegation

  1. Alpha first: the simplest thing that works. When two answers fit, prefer the simpler.
  2. Nothing persists: no database, no accounts; a restart wiping everything is fine.
  3. Use what is at hand and defer scale: Bento and Node now, faster servers later.
  4. One path for everything: rooms and Frontline share one server and one rule set.
  5. Zero friction for the player: one click to be in a fight.
  6. Normal rules, with resources as the only balance lever.

Taste shown by the corrections

  • Time is not a requirement. A long game means more points to win, nothing else.
  • Nothing runs for nobody. A Frontline game exists only while a human is in it.
  • Open is not AI. In a room, an empty seat stays empty; AI is something the host chooses.
  • Drop-in rules stay in the drop-in mode. Rooms are ordinary matches with friends.
  • In battle you are always blue, at the bottom. The lobby never flips.

The sweep covered the browser's battle client and lobby (about 25 files), the simulation's battle, economy, objectives, AI and publication code, and the repo's build, deploy and check setup.

1. Battles slow down the longer they run decided: 54 open: O3

Evidence. Units are only ever appended (crates/sim/src/battle/dispatch.rs:237); about a dozen per-tick loops walk every unit, dead or retired (crates/sim/src/battle.rs:1717, 2190, 2739, 4113). Wrecks, corpses, the recorded order log and the AI's receipts are never trimmed. Each move order clones the whole unit list (battle.rs:3657).

Why it bites. A 35-minute match never notices. A multi-hour game with purchases that never stop pays for every unit ever bought, every tick.

What it changes. Frontline's target starts small. Making the sim forget the dead is its own piece of work before long games.

2. Aiming asks the battle, every time the mouse moves decided: 55

Evidence. The move ghost and the purchase ghost are answered by the battle, re-asked whenever the cursor or the tick changes, limited only to one in flight (web/src/battle/input/orderPreview.ts:79, purchasePlacement.ts:77). Each answer runs path searches over cloned units (crates/sim/src/battle/move_placement.rs:84).

Why it bites. Online it is server work per player per mouse move, with no rate limit, and a ghost that trails the cursor by a round trip. A purchase can't be confirmed until its answer matches the cursor.

3. One page can freeze or spy on the battle decided: 56

Evidence. The page sends pause on Esc and on every tab switch (web/src/battle/sim/client.ts:344), starts the clock, and can ask for another side's view (authority.ts:290) or the full order log.

4. The stream can't skip a record, and a snapshot is shared decided: 57

Evidence. A skipped record is an error that fails the battle on the page (crates/sim/src/publication/reader.rs:308). The WebAssembly handle holds one publisher (crates/game-wasm/src/lib.rs:647), and a change of side empties its baseline (publication.rs:533).

Why it bites. Serving both sides from today's binding sends a full snapshot every tick. A per-side publisher means one player's resync reaches their teammates too. The page's three-buffer credit scheme would stall a server at any round trip over 100 ms.

5. Nobody has ever commanded Red decided: 64, 65

Evidence. The battle route passes no side or player and defaults to Blue (web/src/battle/useBattleSession.ts:104). The camera opens on the first human's road (crates/sim/src/entries.rs:134). Scores, objective rings and the result screen colour by Blue and Red (web/src/hud.css:1593, battleOverlay.ts:421). Every human banner reads "You" (Lobby.tsx:303). Links refuse a human on Red (battleLinks.ts:181).

6. The lobby code can't run on a server as it stands decided: 58

Evidence. The lobby model reaches bundler-only imports and the renderer through battleLinks.ts and skirmishSetup.ts. Path aliases exist only in Vite and tsc. Lint, format and type-checking cover packages apps web/src only, and all JavaScript dependencies live in web/package.json.

7. Every staging build stays live decided: 59

Evidence. Vercel keeps each staging deployment reachable and promotes one without a rebuild. A build can read no environment and no server address. The sim's build id is not exported; buildIdentity.ts hashes the served simulation file. The Vercel install doesn't pin wasm-pack, so two builds of one commit may differ.

8. Frontline's seat rule needs two new battle mechanics decided: 6, 29

Evidence. Nothing moves a unit, a wallet or a pending purchase between seats today, and nothing resets a seat. Refunds pay the original buyer (battle.rs:595); purchase numbers are per player and can clash (battle.rs:1306); the only cap check is on buying (skirmish.rs:274).

Why it bites. The receiving AI can hold up to 150 units: it stops buying, and it hits the 30-orders-a-second limit per player. Both mechanics need a recorded event, like the controller change has, to replay exactly.

9. Native and WebAssembly end on different digests open: O2

Evidence. The same battle ends on 63ca37e9b78dcd8a natively (Mac and Linux) and 421fb87634122caa in WebAssembly (Mac and Linux). After one simulated minute, before any shooting, both are 217810a3a7c10c42.

Why it bites. It does not block a server-run battle. It does contradict the repo's parity tests for longer fights, and a server battle's replay may not match in a native tool.

Sharp edges

  • Bento's machine is slow per core. About four times slower than the Mac for native code; 23 to 24% late ticks in the 200-unit fight, native or Node. O1
  • Bento's edge forwards its own credentials in request headers to the app. The server must never log or echo request headers.
  • Unit motion is timed by when records arrive (client.ts:277), so network jitter shows as uneven movement.
  • An AI taking over a mid-fight army is untested. One returning to a seat reads the changed army as losses and may fall back. O5
  • An AI seat with no units rebuilds at one purchase every 5 seconds and holds its side's push back for up to 30 seconds.
  • Memory. One battle used 556 MiB after ten minutes; a large map's ground alone can reach 300 MB. Memory may cap battles before cores do.
  • A battle that crashes is lost. A corpse sort panics on a bad position (battle.rs:4139); nothing recovers a battle.
  • Orders are slow through Bento's public route. About 300 ms per order or preview from California, against about 100 ms on the private route; Bento's docs say the public host is checked on every request. Players farther from Virginia will see more. The stream down is unaffected. O6
  • Refused and rate-limited orders are still recorded, so a hostile page can grow a battle's order log.

Small facts for the builder to confirm before coding

  • Seat ids are positions today (blue-1), so removing a seat renumbers the rest. Online needs a seat identity that doesn't move.
  • The link parser and the lobby model refuse more than one human, and any human on Red. Relax both on purpose.
  • A controller change does not touch a seat's ready flag; the preparation phase ends when everyone is ready or after 60 seconds.
  • The result screen needs only the final observation, not the replay.
  • Leaving sends no message today; the server learns of it when the stream closes.
  • The double right-click that upgrades a move names the first click's order, so the two must arrive in order.
  • useBattleSession.ts is already over the file-size budget: it may shrink but not grow.
  • Every response needs Cache-Control: no-store; Bento's public cache otherwise keeps a response for 60 seconds.
  • Binary streams are cut at 300 seconds and event streams at 240.
  • The server must load the very simulation file the site serves, not its own build of it.
  • Lane numbers run from one fixed end of the map. With your side always at the bottom in battle, check how they read for a Red commander.
  • The probe app mp-probe is still registered, private and idle, in the armata workspace.

Judgment calls

Frontline's battle rules

The points target, the instant win turned off, closing at 90%, the fresh start and the army hand-over. Alternatives already weighed: a joiner inheriting the seat as it stands (no new mechanics), and a catch-up bonus for the trailing side.

How aiming feels online

Server previews capped at five a second; through Bento's public route about three a second is all a round trip allows. The alternative, if the lag is unpleasant, is answering previews in the browser from what the player's own side knows.

Reconnects and snapshots

One publisher per side with a short backlog. The alternative is a publisher per player, which costs memory and saves teammates from each other's resyncs.

The room and Frontline screens

From the mock, built with the game's own lobby components. The briefing card and the room row are the two new surfaces.

Commanding Red

Always blue and always at the bottom in battle. The lobby stays as it is: sides left and right.

Mechanical work

Foundation
  • A server folder that joins lint, type-checking and tests; the lobby model split free of bundler-only imports.
  • A per-side publisher binding in the WebAssembly build.
  • The battle host on a worker thread: clock, orders in, records out, never waiting on a player.
  • The remote channel in the browser, behind the existing client seam, with resume by record number.
  • Callsign and browser id; build identity check; the Bento deploy from the site's commit.
Rooms
  • Room list and lobby event streams; create, join by code, seat moves, open or AI, ready, host hand-over, start.
  • Seats closing up at the start; drop and return to your own seat.

  • Placement, creating and deleting games, the idle rule.
  • The two recorded mechanics, each with its tests, through the mechanics skill.
