# S2: what the three building graphs give us

**Status:** run on 2026-10-01, headless in Blender 5.2.1, on the three vendored files (`packages/scene-assets/blender/vendor/procedural-buildings/`). 58 Paris, 84 New York and 74 China recipes were swept. The scripts and raw tables were scratch and are not kept; the numbers below are the conclusions.

## The answer

Each source can be exported as a small kit of shared modules plus placement rows for each template, and the kit does not grow with the number of templates. Their coverage is narrow: they are apartment blocks. The other categories need our own sources.

| | Paris | New York | China |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instances readable before they are realized | Yes | Yes | Yes, with two Realize Instances nodes muted in memory (`CN_Facade` "Realize Instances.001", `CN_Building` "Realize Instances.003") |
| Shared kit over every recipe | 16 modules, 9,930 triangles | 85 modules, about 36,000 | 90 modules, 49,118 |
| Geometry made new for each recipe | A 12-triangle roof cap | 830 to 11,676 triangles (cornice, band courses, room boxes), plus a party-wall box and the sign | 10,796 to 24,671 triangles (walls with window holes, ground floor, bands, a rooftop cable and sign) |
| Placement rows per building | 76 to 1,640 | 544 to 6,208 | 128 to 5,473 |
| Row transforms | Translation and quarter turns, scale 1 | General rotation; 98% of rows scaled per axis (unit cubes stretched into walls and trim, up to 24 times); a few mirrored | General rotation; 73% scaled per axis (0.04 to 5.6) |
| Shear | None | None | None |
| Facades | All four | Two; the other two are blank party walls, and no input changes that | All four |
| Floor heights | 4.2 m ground, 3.2 m above, a mansard on top, fixed | Settable; wall top is exactly 3 m a floor | Settable; wall top is 3 m a floor plus 0.2 m, and a 1.1 m parapet |
| Evaluation | 2 to 8 ms | 10 to 92 ms | 9 to 51 ms |
| Same inputs, same output | Yes, in one process and across two | Yes | Yes |

**One footprint rule fits all three:** a side of 3n + 2 metres has n bays at exactly 3.0 m, with bay centres 2.5 + 3k metres from the corner. The local origin is the street corner on the ground, and the walls follow the inputs exactly. Overhangs past the walls: Paris 0.9 m, New York up to 1.8 m, China up to 1.55 m.

**Materials.** Paris and New York texture by world-space projection in the shader and their modules have no UVs. China's kit meshes carry UVs in metres. China's per-instance wall tint and New York's per-instance blind, sign and prop colours live in instance attributes, outside the transform. China's vertex-colour alpha marks "takes the tint", which in our pipeline would read as wear (L9). The texture paths are relative and resolve beside the files; only the embedded generator scripts hard-code the author's machine.

**Sharp edges for the exporter.** Inputs are not clamped through Python and the files are saved in non-default states (New York's `Building Height` is saved at 29.12 and overrides the floor inputs until set to 0), so every input is set explicitly from the file's saved values each time. Menu inputs take the item's identifier string. Street outputs are inputs to switch off (New York `Sidewalk`; China `Sidewalk`, `Tactile Paving`, `Street Trees`, `Street Lamps`, `Street Props`); Paris's sidewalk is separate objects that are simply not exported. Neither graph's own detail input gets near a far tier.

## Which category each source covers

| Category | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Urban apartment (4 to 8 floors) | **Covered.** China fits free-standing slabs and point blocks best. New York fits a street-corner block. Paris fits only if its taller floors are the template's own heights. |
| Highrise (9 floors and up) | A taller apartment block at best (China to 16 floors, New York to 20). None is a tower. C18's own source stands. |
| Attached home | **Not covered.** No source makes a two-storey house or a mid-terrace unit with one street face. New York makes a believable three-floor corner shop. |
| Detached home, farmstead, industry | Not covered, as expected (C16, C17, C19). |

## Verdicts (the G0 rows this spike owed)

- **G0-EXPORT: modules and placement rows, read before realization.** The format is in [the city kit readme](../../../packages/scene-assets/blender/city/README.md). A row is a module, a position, a yaw, a scale per axis, the detail tiers it draws at, and a tint. Anything a row cannot say (a tilt, a mirror) is baked into a module variant at export.
- **G0-STRETCH: generated primitives are folded into the template's own mesh.** A stretched unit cube cannot carry a baked texture, so rows of generated primitives (walls, frames, rails) are realized into one mesh per template with UVs in metres. Real kit meshes stay instanced, with their scale in the row.
- **G0-DIMENSIONS: sides are 3n + 2 metres and floors 3 m,** so the simulation's 3 m bays and floors hold for every graph-made template. Physical templates made from a graph take their size from its recipe, and the catalogue's hash moves when they land.
- **G0-FAMILY: one regional family ships first, and it is China's.** It fits the generator's free-standing slabs, has four facades, and its lived-in balconies are the look the user named. New York and Paris follow through the same exporter as further families; a map uses one family.
- **G0-AUTHORED: every category the graphs do not cover is our own scripted source,** and that now includes attached homes (C17 makes detached and attached houses). They are members of each shipped family until a family has its own.
- **G0-JOINS (S5) is not settled here.** Compound templates (the U and courtyard blocks, terraces) are tried when the first single-part templates draw; until then a compound keeps its prototype rows.
- **O-2 is answered:** a kit is 16 to 90 modules and fits one bundle.
