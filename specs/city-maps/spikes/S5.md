# S5: joins between a template's parts

**Status:** answered in production on 2026-10-02 by two shipped templates of the China set, `china-apartment-block-u-5f` and `china-apartment-block-court-6f` (`packages/scene-assets/blender/city/china.py`). Nothing here is a throwaway.

## The answer

A join can be hidden completely, and no variant, pilaster or corner module is needed, because a join is never built. A template is boxes that abut; the exporter traces the outline of their union and builds only that. Each straight run of the outline is one facade of the graph, evaluated at the run's length, so its bays, corner piers and shops are those of one wall. The bands, the parapet and its coping are rings round the outline, mitred at every corner, convex or concave. Each part's roof is one quad, and the quads meet edge to edge under one parapet.

The approach named first, evaluating the graph per wing and dropping what lies on a joined face, was not built. A wing's facade beside a join would be one facade cut mid-bay: with any legal wing width the cut falls 1 m into a bay, through a window.

## What is supported

| Shape | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Any union of boxes that abut along faces, all of one height: L, U, T, a closed court | **Supported.** U and court are shipped; L and T follow from the same rule and were not exported. |
| A run of the outline of 3n + 2 m or 3n m | **Supported**, with 1 m and 1.5 m corner piers. |
| A run of 3n + 1 m | **Refused by the exporter.** Its piers would be 2 m, and the descriptor's 3 m lattice would claim a bay inside each. So a compound's wings are 12 m deep: with the slabs' 11 m or 14 m the run between two wings is always 3n + 1. |
| Parts that touch only at a corner | **Refused.** There is no outline to follow. |
| Parts of different heights, a wing set at an angle, terraces of attached houses | **Not tried.** A step in the roof needs a wall above the lower roof that no facade of the graph makes. Terraces are the houses' own source. |

## The descriptor

A joined span of a part's face is an edge with `exposed: false` and no bays, and `joins` pairs it with the span it meets, exactly as the prototype rows did. An exposed edge's lattice is checked against the windows the graph put on it: one run of the outline crosses several parts' edges (the U's west flank is the front's gable and the west wing's side) and its windows land on every lattice point of each, sometimes half a metre from where two parts meet. The exporter stops if a window sits on a joined span, on a boundary between parts, or off the lattice.

Entrances are on the southmost run, which takes the graph's entrance facade, so a compound has one door and its way out is clear of its own wings.

## What the pictures showed

Renders of the reassembled set at 30, 80 and 250 m and close on each kind of join (scratch, not kept):

- **Outer wall across a join** (the U's flank): one facade. Bands, parapet and stone base run through; there is no pier, seam or change of rhythm where the front ends and the wing begins.
- **Inner corner:** two walls meeting as in any building. Bands turn the corner mitred. Balconies and air conditioners keep clear of it: the last bay is 2.5 m or 3 m from the corner.
- **Roof across a join:** one tiled surface, the tile pattern continuous (UVs are in the template's frame), one parapet round the block and one round the yard. Each part keeps a stair house of its own, which is the only thing that counts the wings.
- **At the coarse tiers** the flat walls and the same rings are used; nothing changes at a join.

An unprimed critique, asked where separate buildings had been joined, found no seam, step or doubled cornice in either block, at the fine tier or the coarse one, and read each as one building. What it could point to was the facade's own rhythm (the graph's columns of small windows, which fall anywhere) and two things that are not joins: where both walls of an inner corner end in a balcony on a 3n + 2 run, the balconies' corners overlap by 9 cm; and the graph hangs an air conditioner hard against the next bay's balcony, on the slabs as here.

## Limits found on the way

- The court has 408 bays and one template's triangle budget, so it is smaller than the prototype it replaces (41 x 38 m round a 17 x 14 m yard, not 44 x 40 m).
- With rooms left out until C26, the kit's open casement window let the eye through a 12 m wing and out the other side. Every opening is now lined with a dark pane inside the wall ([C14's pass](../slices/C14-damage-placements.md)).
