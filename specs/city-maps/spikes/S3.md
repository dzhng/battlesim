# S3: a generated map in the browser (first pass)

**Status:** first measurements on 2026-10-01, on the lab route `/lab/generated` (`?type=open|mixed|metro&size=small|medium|large&seed=`). Every type at Small and Large, and Mixed and Metro at Medium, generates in a worker, starts a battle and draws at 47 FPS or better in every view tried, on this Mac (Apple Metal). Buildings are prototype massing; the forest, roads, fog and ground are the village's. The tool is the scratch `throwaway/measure.mjs` (not committed); the regression check is the `generated` scene.

## The question

What breaks when a full-size generated map is prepared, started and drawn in the browser: does it load, how long does it take, what does a frame cost, and what does it hold in memory?

## Does it load

Yes, at every size, with no limit raised. What made that possible was already in: sparse terrain export (SA1), sparse ground and scar pages (SA3), incremental fog delivery (SA4) and the shared planning grid (SA2). Nothing hit a GPU limit: the height buffer, the scar pool (8 MiB), the fog's structure table (13,579 boxes in a 512-cell grid) and the instance buffers all fit.

Three things were not failures to load but made the route unusable, and one made the overview unreadable. All four are fixed in this pass (see below).

## What broke, and what fixed it

- **The forest drew whole into every pass.** It was never culled, because trees cast shadows into view from off screen: 80,869 trees at 80 triangles into the prepass, the colour pass and four cascades, 14 ms of GPU from any camera. Now only chunks in view are drawn, and a chunk out of view is drawn into the cascades only where its shadow can land in view. 14 ms became 3 to 4.5 ms at the tactical camera.
- **The grass build asked every prop on the map about every clump.** 94,000 props on Metro Large; the grass regrows whenever the ground changes, so a moving unit cost 17 to 22 ms a frame with 100 ms spikes. It now reads its grid cell's list.
- **The plot split tested every road against every plot** and every building against every leaf: 19 s of a 22 s start. A plot now keeps only the roads whose box meets its own. The plot tree is byte-identical.
- **The whole-map view was white.** The haze was tuned for a camera that stops at 2 km. Past that orbit distance the haze's distances now stretch with the camera's, and the lab's camera reaches 1.5 map widths.
- **Every building wore the village's house, stretched.** Prototype buildings are now massing boxes in the scenery layer's static chunks (13,579 boxes on Metro Large are 163,000 triangles in a few draw ranges), and the house art is not loaded.

## What remains

- **The page builds a second world** (C33): part of the 1.4 s between "prepared" and "world built" and of the page's 550 MiB on Metro Large; neither share was measured apart. The scenario text (27 MB there) is also parsed twice on the page.
- **The map is generated twice** in the worker, once for the map and once for the plan (0.7 s on Metro Large), until the planner (C59) says what it reads.
- **No far tier** (C23): the whole-map view draws every tree at 80 triangles, 9 to 11 ms on Large. This is the view nearest the floor.
- **Fog at scale** (C20): each rebuilt sight map tests every known occluder on every one of its 4,096 rays, and each publication samples every structure's walls. With nine eyes on Metro Large that is about 1 ms; it grows with eyes times structures and was not measured with a hundred units a side.
- **A ground change walks every tree** to see which still stand (0.8% of the main thread while units move), and on a map too big for one scar cache the ground draws once per 1 km region in view (a hundred regions at the whole-map view of Large, inside the "moving, whole map" numbers).
- **Corpse chunks** have not moved onto the static chunk path (the rest of C22).
- **The camera can enter a building** (C57), and a street draws as the village's dirt road (C28).
- **The encounter is a stand-in** (C58, C59): fixed placements read off the plan, with no check of range or cover.
- **Not measured:** Open Medium; a quiet machine; a start with a cold disk cache; a hundred units a side.

## What an unprimed critic saw

Seventeen 1920 × 1080 shots and twelve crops (Mixed Small, Metro Small, Metro Large; the column, the town at 65 m and 400 m, a street, the whole map; as played and with fog off) went to a critic with no backstory. It judged the 400 m town view, the street, the column and both whole-map views readable, and the town at the default 65 m camera not: four or five roofs fill the frame.

Fixed here, because they made the frame hard to read:
- **Town roofs and straw fields shared an orange, and pale homes read as light fields** from the whole-map view. The tints moved off the fields' palette (brick, off-white, greys).
- **Neighbouring boxes of one category merged into one flat mass**, most of all on their shaded side. Each box now takes its own value, within 8% of its tint.
- **No shot showed a tree.** A forest edge was added to the evidence: crowns on trunks over the forest floor.

Left, and whose they are:
- **Broken white arcs over the town.** They are the hold zone's ring, painted on the ground and so hidden wherever a building stands. An objective mark that reads among buildings is the encounter's (C58) and game-ui's.
- **Sun shadows inside fog are the darkest thing in a fully fogged frame** and could read as a deeper fog; boxes cast large hard ones. The hatch moires with the fields from the whole-map height, and the sight outline shows a white sliver for every gap a unit sees through. All three are the fog look's (C20 and the visual pass); the first is the standing fog-versus-shadow rule and should be looked at first.
- **The whole-map haze reads as a wall at each side:** haze is by distance from the camera's focus, and the frame is wider than it is tall.
- **Faint creases and wedges of another green across town ground:** the field patchwork runs under the town (C31).
- **Blocky shadow edges at 400 m, a courtyard that reads as a tilted roof, one needle-thin tree at a forest's edge, grass blades that stop at a road, labels that stack at a low camera:** all as in the village; none is new here.
