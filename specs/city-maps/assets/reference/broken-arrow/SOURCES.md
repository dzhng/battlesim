## What they show

**`town-districts` (low oblique over a town):**
- Districts are single-use areas side by side: a garden suburb of detached houses on looser streets; apartment slabs in parallel rows with lawns and parking; one tall tower; an industrial compound and a container yard with large paved aprons.
- A straight main road and a rail line run through the middle as the town's spine; the yard and industry sit along it.
- Tree lines and small woods separate districts, and fields and forest begin at the last row of houses.
- Capture zones sit on districts (the industrial compound, the apartment block), not on empty ground.

**`town-overview` (higher, wider):**
- The town is a loose aggregate strung along the road and rail corridor, with fields and dense forest wedges between its districts.
- A warehouse compound stands apart at the edge, on its own access road.
- Dense, irregular forest patches interleave with open farmland all the way to the horizon; smaller clusters of houses sit around the main town.

## Open-country reference target

The new originals are the user's Desktop files `Screenshot 2026-10-02 at 12.14.07 PM.png` (3024 × 1912) and `Screenshot 2026-10-02 at 12.14.27 PM.png` (3024 × 1898), copied without resizing. Fields remain the dominant open spaces. Short tree belts, narrow wooded boundaries, irregular copses, small groups of houses and isolated compounds give the country landmarks between larger settlements. They make the next open stretch legible and put physical interruptions in some sight lines without enclosing every field in a continuous forest or hedge.

The user's requirement is the existing M24/M25 rule: a starting unit should have some interrupted sight bearings, while most of its view stays open. These screenshots are the composition target, not proof of this game's fog behavior. Buildings and trees must affect the simulation's sight where they are drawn; low rocks, logs and other field clutter add character and cover where their physical properties allow it. Preserve the main settlement's long weapon approaches.

Use both complete views when judging the generated map's countryside at tactical and overview zoom. Judge spacing, variety and the relationship between fields, lanes, tree belts and rural building groups. Do not copy the video's HUD, streamer overlay, river shape or exact geography. The river banks and forests may inspire composition; terrain relief remains outside city-maps.
