# Infantry weapon use

A soldier works one weapon at a time. His carried launcher remains available, but it does not grant a second pair of hands. Vehicles keep independent mounts. When the launcher cannot engage and the rifle has a legal target, its carrier uses his rifle and wears the launcher on his back.

## Why ownership and use are separate

A launcher belongs to a physical weapon and passes to a survivor. Selecting a rifle must neither lose that launcher nor replace its magazine. Selection therefore belongs to the soldier, while magazine, reload and aim belong to each physical weapon. The shared squad targeting lock chooses a target; it cannot own the switcher’s aim without also resetting his guards.

Selection reserves the operator before physical cycles advance: existing guidance takes precedence, and a useful launcher keeps the launch tick exclusive, including after casualty handoff. Otherwise a useful assigned single weapon has priority even while aiming or reloading. Picking only ready weapons would put away the launcher every tick and prevent its reload from finishing. Switching has no equip timer, but ordinary aim still applies. Inactive reload work pauses; its magazine remains, aim and burst-start work clear, and cooldown elapses. Stationary movement interruption retains its existing rule.

Building windows use that same priority and normal target selection, evaluated at prospective windows. Idle window watching permits threat assessment while holding fire only when there are no known attackers; this grants no firing permission. Each soldier requests one weapon’s window: competing rifle and launcher requests otherwise pull the gunner away from his target. Requests retain authored squad/single ownership because a last surviving rifleman must not acquire a launcher’s priority. Current seat availability cannot be the prerequisite for requesting a suitable seat. Window allocation still works by facing facade, rather than searching alternate seats on the same facade for better trajectories.

## One presentation decision

Visible member identity carries active mount alongside slot. Carrier assignment remains separate; both facts follow the observation’s visibility filter. The renderer chooses model, clips, facing and muzzle from those facts, without guessing from reload or historical squad shots. Authoritative activity and physical aim enter the battle digest.

The procedural art uses paired whole-body active/carried sets. Variant index preserves identity across the launcher and rifle holds, and each hold brings its own clips and muzzle socket. This extends the existing whole-body asset system rather than introducing runtime weapon attachments. The authored object is a hard cutover with no array compatibility parser.

Equipment changes reset incompatible blending. Death uses ordinary kit so the recovered launcher is not drawn on both bodies. Delayed launch evidence remains attached to its mount; a lingering flash from a weapon no longer held uses its published launch point. Acquiring a launcher while continuing to fire a rifle preserves that rifle’s animation evidence.

## Comparisons preserve their subject

A weapon’s target-priority claim applies within that weapon’s own range; rifle reach cannot stand in for grenade reach. Simulation-backed visual staging requires admitted destinations and publication through their application tick, rather than assuming a requested fight began. Material comparisons hold illumination fixed, and building-presence checks compare the same projected region with buildings shown and hidden. These controls preserve the physical and visible requirements while excluding unrelated staging failures.

The [fine-route owner](../../../crates/sim/src/movement/final_leg.rs) can prove a clear connector without searching, but must preserve the original search’s waypoint result and use its fallback whenever clearance is uncertain. Logical search accounting follows actual searches. The [comparison controller](../../../crates/sim/src/village/scripts.rs) spreads each returning squad’s destination around a shared point; sending all healed squads to one occupied settlement tests refusal rather than rejoining. Paired comparisons must use the same controller on both arms.
