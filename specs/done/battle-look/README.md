# Battle look: a playable summer village in the WARNO style

**Shipped (2026-09-28).** The village battle (`/battle/village`) is playable from the main menu in the new look, with both armies and every unit type the catalog holds, at 81.6 FPS average over the benchmark's full run on the target Mac against the 30 FPS floor (the "final" row of [`frame-cost.md`](frame-cost.md)).

What shipped:
- **The look.** A summer patchwork of painterly fields, dense wind-bent 3D grass, trees and hedgerows, a warm afternoon light with cascaded sun shadows and an AgX grade, battle scars on the ground, and fire, smoke, dust and tracers.
- **Fog of war you can read.** Sharp, building-cut sight on screen that never reads as sun shadow.
- **Real models.** Textured, animated modern soldiers and articulated vehicles, wrecks and ruins, all generated by code.
- **The rules underneath.** Directional sight, an animation feed, ricochets, an authoritative ground layer, Company of Heroes-style per-soldier movement and cover, weight and pushing, destroyable props, leaning out round cover, and synthesised sound.
- **Game UI.** Total War camera and keys, holo-tactical in-world orders, a strategy-game HUD, a main menu and a scripted benchmark.

It mixes several references: framing and scars from Defilade, effects from Broken Arrow and WARNO, and sharp urban fog of war from ARMAPHRACT. The references are under [Where the standard came from](#where-the-standard-came-from).

This folder is the record of **why** it is built this way. The code is the record of **how**; each section names where to look.

## Why it works this way

- **The simulation stays the one authority.** Every new look needed a rule the simulation publishes, never a guess the renderer makes:
  - which way a unit looks (`sim::sight`);
  - what each soldier is doing (the animation feed in `sim::publication`);
  - where a round went after it glanced (`sim::flight`);
  - what the ground has become (`sim::ground`).

  So a replay reproduces the picture as well as the outcome, and presentation can never show what a side doesn't know.
- **Fog is judged against light.** The hardest standing rule is that *fog must never read as sun shadow, nor shadow as fog*. It could only be judged once sun shadows existed, so fog followed light. Every visual gate asked an unprimed critic that exact question. It shaped the fog's ruled lines and cool tint, the rim on the seen side of the edge, a shadow floor that keeps shade lit, and a forest floor that never reads as unseen ground ([`fog and light`](../../../.agents/skills/renderer/references/fog-and-light.md)).
- **Sight lights, chosen by measurement.** Each own eye gets a polar horizon map, culled per screen tile (`frame/fogVisibility.ts`). Spike 02 measured it against a 1 m viewshed: the viewshed stair-steps building edges 5–7 px at ground framing by construction. Sight lights keep stair-stepping under half a pixel, with the edge within about 1.4 px of the true corner line, agree with the simulation's sweep on all but a fraction of a percent of cells, and cost under 2 ms per frame ([`spikes/02.md`](spikes/02.md)).
- **The renderer is ported, not copied.** `~/dev/game`'s TypeGPU battle renderer (shadows, sky, terrain, grass, the skinned crowd, the bundle pipeline) was the source. Its import closure was about 25k lines, so spike 01 pruned it to 3.7k and each file entered by one of three modes:
  - a verbatim copy;
  - an adapted copy;
  - a technique rewritten from reading.

  Each is recorded with its own source commit in the reuse manifest ([`reuse-manifest.json`](../../../reuse-manifest.json)). Nothing imports the sibling repo at runtime ([`spikes/01.md`](spikes/01.md)).
- **Art is generated by code, and fitted to the simulation's numbers.** Headless Blender scripts and numpy texture recipes build every model and texture. The bake packs them into binary, content-addressed bundles, with animation stored once per skeleton. `~/dev/game`'s JSON-float encoding had reached 3.3 GB. Hull extents, eye heights and every mount's pivot and muzzle are simulation data. The validator's `fit.*` checks hold the art to them, never the other way round ([`packages/scene-assets`](../../../packages/scene-assets/README.md)).
- **Mechanics come from bodies, not names.** The movement lane was pulled into scope mid-build (user, 2026-09-26). It follows the root README's [rules from first principles](../../../README.md#rules-from-first-principles):
  - obstacles are body rows (blocks, stops rounds, weight, cover tier, integrity);
  - movers have footprints and push classes;
  - no code asks "is this a wall?".

  The same rule later turned unit and prop types into catalog data: behaviour comes from a type's components, never its id ([`fixtures/README.md`](../../../fixtures/README.md)).
- **Hollywood realism, not ballistics.** Where a principled rule looked silly on screen, a plain game rule replaced it. This spec added one: a soldier's own rounds pass the cover he leans round. The root README keeps the list of hard-coded rules, and nowhere else does.

## Invariants (what must keep holding)

- **Replay and digest parity.** New authoritative state, and each side's knowledge, enters `Battle::digest` (`crates/sim/src/battle.rs`). A performance change leaves every digest unchanged, or is a named decision in [`decisions.md`](decisions.md) with its effect on outcomes measured. The 27 perf decisions are the precedent.
- **Presentation reads only the published observation**, plus public static geometry. It never reads simulation state, and it poses soldiers from their published positions and ids, never from formation slots.
- **One owner per concept** (the table below), and **hard cutover**: a replacement deletes the old path in the same change, with no compatibility layer. Unit proxies, the flat-shaded frame, per-kind muzzles, the enum of named prop kinds (`PropKind` is now a catalog index) and trenches all went this way.
- **Every tunable number is in data, validated by the module that reads it.** That covers the rules in `fixtures/village.json`, unit and prop types in the catalog, the biome in `fixtures/biomes/summer.json`, and presentation in the fixture's `presentation` block. The one named exception is how far art may sit from the simulation's numbers: that is a catalog tolerance in `assets/catalog.json`, because it is a check on art, not a rule.
- **Fog never reads as shadow.** Seen pixels are never dimmed or tinted by fog; only the edge's rim line lies on the seen side. Units are drawn by the simulation's identification, not by the fog, and are never fogged. The drawn fog and the simulation's sweep use one set of eyes (`sensing::eyes`); the fog scene checks they agree outside a one-cell band on at least 95% of 8 m cells.
- **Pixel checks are retuned one at a time, each with a `decisions.md` entry saying why the old bar was wrong.** They are never loosened silently. Model sheets are judged "less wrong" by critique, never by pixel goldens, because hardware Metal renders are not bit-stable.
- **The playable floor is 30 FPS average at the default camera, at 1920×1080 on this Mac, measured by the benchmark.** It is the only frame budget. The general performance budget is a later spec.
- **Every third-party byte is in the reuse manifest with an allow-listed licence** (the validator refuses anything else). References never ship.

## How each slice was gated

Later specs inherit these gates:
- **Contracts.** Replay and digest parity stay green. The first tests are red/green at the seam, run with the narrowest runner. `bun run check` and `bun run verify` run once, at the end of a pass.
- **Frame cost.** Every slice records a row in `frame-cost.md`, from the benchmark's short run: frame time p50/p95/p99, the GPU frame total (Apple's tile GPU overlaps passes, so there is no reliable per-pass split), buffer and texture bytes, and publication bytes.
- **Visual slices.** Each names its one visual variable and a reference crop. It judges less-wrong against the crop with [compare-screenshots](../../../.agents/skills/compare-screenshots/SKILL.md), not by pixel match. Its last check is an unprimed [screenshot-critique](../../../.agents/skills/screenshot-critique/SKILL.md) that asks whether any dark region could be mistaken for sun shadow, or any shadow for fog. Then comes a non-blocking [preview-shots](../../../.agents/skills/preview-shots/SKILL.md) checkpoint: if the user doesn't answer in about five minutes, the agent decides on the evidence and records the call.
- **Movement slices** verify through the scenario runner (`crates/sim/examples/movement_shots.rs`). It renders dots-and-boxes GIFs that the agent reviews, and the user looks last.

## Single owners

| Concept | Owner |
|---|---|
| Sight shape and range by direction | `sim::sight`. Read by sensing and the fog sweep, and published for the renderer's fog. |
| Which eyes a side sees from | `sensing::eyes`: one list for the sweep, identification and the published `UnitSight::eyes`. A garrison sees from one eye per facade it holds (`garrison::facade_eyes`). |
| What a unit is doing, for animation (soldier ids, weapon pose, shots, blasts, impact detail) | Published by `sim::publication`. Poses are derived only in `battle-renderer`'s pose driver (`models/poseDriver.ts`). |
| Round flight and ricochet | `sim::flight`, whose `ImpactResolver` is implemented once, by `damage` (`HullResolver`): penetration and face policy live there. |
| Ground layer: craters, wear, cleared lanes | Rules in `sim::ground`, per-side learned cells in knowledge, delivery in one patch protocol (`web/src/battle/sim/ground.ts` on the browser side). |
| Unit and prop types | The catalog (`fixtures/units/`, `fixtures/props/`), resolved once by `contract::catalog`. The browser and the asset tools read the generated view `fixtures/catalog.json`. |
| What a body blocks, stops, hides, weighs and covers | The prop type's body row (`PropBody`, carried on each `world::Prop`) and a unit type's `body`. Each column has its own readers. |
| Pushing | `movement::push` (the kinematic shove, box against box), applied by `Battle`. Each side's last-seen poses are in `movement::SideGeometry`. |
| A weapon's muzzle | The unit type's mount rows (`pivot_m`, `muzzle_m`, the carrier `on`), read by `weapons` in the simulation and by `scene-assets/src/mountMuzzle.ts` on the art side. Effects sit on the drawn muzzle (`DrawnMuzzles`) and fall back to the published point. |
| Camera and input | Camera behaviour is in `renderer-core`'s `CameraController`. Command keys are in `web/src/battle/input/commandBindings.ts` (`CommandBindings`). |
| Frame, passes and GPU lifetimes | `battle-renderer`'s frame (`createBattleFrame`), plus one resource registry (`frame/registry.ts`). |
| Fog in pixels | One `FogTerm` in every world material writes a fog mask beside its colour. One fog mask pass (`frame/fogMaskPass.ts`) gives unseen pixels the frame's `FogStyle` (`presentation.fog`, with named styles; the default is `dusk`), softens the edge and draws the rim. |
| In-world marks | Order marks (`orderOverlay.ts`) take each colour role's colour and layer from the fixture's scheme (`presentation.overlay.orders.scheme`). A layer is either overlay (composited after post, glowing by its own halo in `frame/overlayPass.ts`, never bloom) or ground paint (`frame/paintedMarks.ts`, read only by the ground layers, never by bodies). |
| HUD look | `presentation.hud`, applied as CSS custom properties by `web/src/battle/present/hudTheme.ts` for `web/src/hud.css`. |
| Appearance bundles (schema, validation, baking, loading) | `packages/scene-assets`. There is one loader, for the workbench and the battle alike. Every drawn object is an appearance the workbench can show. |
| Biome look | `fixtures/biomes/summer.json`, read by the terrain, grass and trees. |
| TypeScript vector, matrix, shape, noise, random and easing math | The npm `math` package ([`math` skill](../../../.agents/skills/math/SKILL.md)). `web/tests/mathOwner.test.ts` fails on a new hand-rolled vector or matrix helper. |
| Sound | `packages/battle-audio`. `SoundFrame` reads the same feed the effects and poses read, plus the hearing cues. One Web Audio graph (`WebAudioSink`) plays a bank synthesised in code, with mute and volume in `soundSettings`. |
| Performance measurement | The scripted benchmark (`web/src/battle/benchmark/`, `/benchmark`, the `benchmark` scene), recorded in [`frame-cost.md`](frame-cost.md). The simulation's own measure is instructions retired (`endurance_report`, `village_report`). |

## Where the plan diverged

The plan was a slice ladder of spikes, then parallel simulation, controls, asset and renderer lanes, then a playable village. What changed along the way is the part worth keeping.

- **"Toy-like" models were resliced into textures.** Spike 03 proved the rig, the clips and the vehicle articulation, but two unprimed critiques called the soldier and the tank toy-like. The cause was modelling effort, not the rig, so the contract's fallback (another CC0 rig) wouldn't have helped. Vertex colour alone kept reading as toys. Baked albedo, normal and ORM textures cleared the soldiers, the tank, the truck and the props; the wrecks needed deformed geometry instead, and still read toy-like close up until the user closed them as good enough. The standing rule since: models are good enough, and a focused model pass comes later.
- **The tank's gun length is a rule fitted to the art.** Spike 03 found the simulation's cannon muzzle 3 m out while any real tank's gun reaches about 5.9 m. The rule moved to the model, not the other way round; it moved every digest and the village's capture rate.
- **The first fog look failed its own gate.** Dim, cool and ruled with screen-space lines, a sight wedge's *shape* still read as a second blue cast shadow. The fix came in three steps:
  - a screen-space fog mask pass with a soft edge and a rim on the seen side, drawn across the ground only (on every boundary it ringed trunks and roofs), with a lifted `veil` preset that passed the gate;
  - on the lit side, a shadow floor and a forest floor, because the last misses were seen pixels reading as fog;
  - the user then chose `dusk`, dimmer with the rim, over `veil`; `veil` stays a named style.
- **Scars first read as shadow.** A soft dark disc read as a tree's shadow and the scorch wash as cloud shadow and smoke, so scars became shaped relief with soot and flecks. Single craters, tracks and soot pass. Dense crater fields (merged pits read as shadows cast by mounds) and grazing views are deferred: fixing them needs burst centres published, a simulation contract change.
- **Company of Heroes-style movement joined the plan** (user, 2026-09-26). It had been a firewall. It became a lane of its own:
  - a performance investigation;
  - a dots-and-boxes scenario runner whose GIFs the agent reviews;
  - per-soldier bodies and movement;
  - cover tiers;
  - weight and push classes, with a jeep;
  - forests as bodies;
  - destroyable props;
  - tracked versus wheeled steering and reverse;
  - order markers.
- **Trenches were built, then removed** (user, 2026-09-27: "delete the entire concept of trenches"). The general ground-body cover rule stayed, because rubble uses it.
- **In-world UI went through several looks before the user's pick.**
  - The first was thick overlay ribbons.
  - Then a holo-tactical overlay: thin fixed-width lines, circle-and-arrowhead markers, marching chevrons, leader-line callouts, and a strategy-game HUD. The HUD had been a firewall.
  - Then ground paint that glows; grass over it is lit by the paint only near the root, never painted, so no coloured fringe rises out of a stroke.
  - Finally a fixture scheme per colour role. The shipped `yellow-orders` scheme puts order marks in the overlay in true yellow and the selection in amber ground paint.

  Two dead ends are recorded in the renderer skill: a world decal pass that put strokes on hulls, and a marker with no depth test that drew over house corners.
- **Leaning out and fire-through were added late.** A squad owns an area round an anchor that only orders move. Its soldiers claim places and lean points, step out past tall cover to fire a burst, and tuck back in. A gun fires into the first body on its arc only when that body has integrity, doesn't hide what is behind it, and the rounds left can break it; it holds fire for terrain, buildings and walls, bodies with no integrity, and bodies too tough for its rounds. Fences and crates stop no rounds at all: fire passes through and wears them.
- **Unit and prop types became catalog data** (user: "hundreds of types, factions, variants"). A variant is an `extends` plus overrides, and code lists no types. The only digest-visible change was the replay header's `config_digest`, so replays recorded before it no longer load.
- **Each mount fires from its own muzzle.** A per-kind match had fired the tank's roof HMG from the cannon's tip, or from mid-air when it was turned. Mount rows carry pivot and muzzle, and the art is fit-checked against them.
- **Balance was split off.** The rebalance reached the encounter's supported-capture target (7/10) and the user stopped it there. Rules that landed after it (fire-through, leaning, per-mount muzzles, the performance decisions) moved the rate again and were recorded, not rebalanced: balance is its own later spec. The ledger records where it stands.
- **Sound joined the plan** (user) as the last slice before the playable village, with fog of war holding for hearing: a side hears only what its own units could.
- **The workbench grew to every drawn thing**: trees, grass kinds and every prop are appearances it shows, not shader-only scenery.
- **Performance became named decisions.** The endurance battle's instructions were cut in two ways. First, by changes that left every digest unchanged. Then by three user-approved rules that move outcomes by design:
  - a holding squad re-resolves only for nearby changes;
  - a garrison sees from one eye per facade;
  - observers identify on alternate ticks.

## Dead ends (don't re-walk them)

- **Fog:** 100 omnidirectional depth cube maps (unaffordable); a per-fragment march over every unit; the 1 m GPU viewshed (stair-steps by construction); dim-and-cool alone (reads as shadow).
- **Assets:**
  - three.js as a dev-only authoring dependency (impostors are baked by our own renderer instead);
  - JSON-float animation;
  - CMU mocap (its licence is custom, not CC0) and Mixamo (not redistributable);
  - wreck textures (ten rounds failed; deforming the live vehicle's own parts is what passed at battle views);
  - a sooted-camouflage wreck base (the rust tint turned the print into pink blotches);
  - for part-built models (vehicles, buildings, props), decimating tiers instead of authoring them per part. Infantry, one sculpted body, do decimate (`mesh_lods.py`).
- **Planning:** a fewest-slices ten-slice ladder, whose merged slices had decision budgets too large for one pass. Its rule of one visual variable per verdict was kept.
- **Ricochets:** the next-tick child-round fallback was never needed. A kinetic round that fails to pierce glances off within the same tick.
- **Scars:** a noisy-threshold scorch wash, spokes from the slope or the crater direction, a dark collar, and a 2× supersampled scar texture.
- **Performance:** finer prop-index buckets (8 and 16 m were slower) and a 1 m ring for the step-out search (the user declined it).
- **Marks:** a world decal pass and a marker with no depth test (above); an additive glow, which clamps over white and breaks the overlay isolation algebra.

## Where the standard came from

The visual requirement is the reference frames in [`assets/reference/`](assets/reference/). Each folder's `SOURCES.md` says where its frames came from (store pages, trailers, posts). They are copyrighted, kept with the user's permission as targets only, and never ship. What each drove:
- **WARNO** (`warno/`), the user's favourite aesthetic: the patchwork fields, hedgerows, trees, sky and fireballs; the strategic zoom (`gameplay-tutorial-29`), the light and haze grade (`gameplay-tutorial-14`), grass density (`steam-warno-1`), and the tank and truck (`steam-warno-1`, `-13`).
- **Defilade** (`defilade/`): framing (the default camera at 65 m and 0.85 rad, soldiers about 40 px), battle scars, and the form of infantry (`x-saving-wounded-f4`), houses (`trailer-t36s`) and ruins (`x-shader-craters-f4`).
- **Broken Arrow** (`brokenarrow/`), whose graphics the user rates highest: ground framing, fire and smoke, infantry scale (`steam-brokenarrow-7`) and fireballs (`steam-brokenarrow-1`).
- **ARMAPHRACT** (`armaphract/`): sharp building-cut sight, an untouched seen area, a dimmed and cooled unseen one, hatched contacts, and (`x-urban-fog-t9s`) the callouts on leader lines.
- **The user's own** (`user/`): `holo-glow.png`, the holo-tactical target for glow and material, and `current-overlay-2026-09-27.png`, the "way too thick" before-state of the order overlay.

Reference images are Git LFS: `git lfs pull --include="specs/done/battle-look/assets/reference/<game>/**"`.
